# Channel bible — brand & editorial

> Working name **Mote**: science explainers that start from a question you didn't know you had and end
> with a model of the world you can't un-see. Rename freely; the identity below is what matters.

## Promise to the viewer
1. **Every claim is true and traceable.** Each video ships a public sources page with the passage behind
   every factual sentence. If we got something wrong we pin a correction. This is the moat against the
   AI-slop wave: copycats can clone a look, not a research process.
2. **You leave with a model, not trivia.** Each episode replaces one specific misconception with a
   mechanism the viewer could explain to a friend.
3. **We respect your time.** No padding, no fake suspense, no "but first…". The hook pays off.

## Audience
Curious adults and older teens, no assumed background beyond school science. Smart, busy, sceptical of
hype. They'll forgive simplification; they won't forgive being misled.

## Voice & tone
- Warm, curious, precise. A brilliant friend at a kitchen table, not a lecturer, not a hype man.
- Concrete over abstract: numbers people can feel ("a stack of paper reaching the Moon"), everyday
  objects, second person ("you").
- Honest about uncertainty: "we think", "the best current estimate", "scientists still argue about…" —
  when the sources do.
- Wonder is earned by the facts, never asserted ("mind-blowing", "insane" are banned words).
- Humour is light and comes from the material, often via Mote's reactions.

## Format
- 5–10 minute explainers, weekly at most. Depth over volume (YouTube's 2025–26 "inauthentic content"
  rules punish templated mass production channel-wide).
- Structure: cold-open hook (≤30 s) → the misconception → the journey (2–4 steps, each a mini
  reveal) → the twist/climax → resolution (the model) → a short outro that opens the next question.
- Signature beats: the **question card** in the cold open, **the number** (one quantified payoff the
  viewer will remember), and Mote's reaction shots.

## Editorial standards
- Primary sources first (papers, datasets, agencies, textbooks). Encyclopaedias and journalism to
  orient, never as the only support for a non-trivial claim.
- Quote, don't paraphrase, in claims.json. Scripts may simplify; they may not distort. When a
  simplification is load-bearing, say so on screen or in the sources page ("strictly speaking…").
- No fabricated quotes, people, studies, or footage. No AI likeness of real people. No photoreal
  generative imagery presented as real events.
- Titles and thumbnails must be true to the video. Curiosity gaps are fine; lies are not.
- Corrections: pinned comment + description note within 48 h of a confirmed error; script and sources
  page updated in the repo.

## Mote (the guide character)
A speck of curiosity: a round, sun-yellow mote with big eyes and a sparkle. Mote **reacts** — wonder,
confusion, delight — and asks the viewer's question out loud via the narrator. Mote never states facts
on its own authority and never appears in more than ~30 % of shots. Moods: happy, curious, surprised,
thinking, worried. `Kit.mascot` draws the default Mote; in a scene, Mote takes the style frame's look.
