# Topic backlog

Candidates for the pilot and next episodes. Scores are the topic-scout's first guess (/25 =
question + misconception + originality + payoff + fit, each /5) and must be re-checked with a real
originality survey before an episode starts. See `skills/editorial-framing/SKILL.md`.

## Why don't we feel Earth spinning at ~1,670 km/h?  — 21/25
- Misconception: "we'd feel it / be flung off"; or "it's because gravity holds us down" (half true).
- The number: the centripetal acceleration at the equator is ~0.3 % of g.
- Angle: you *do* feel it — you weigh ~0.3 % less at the equator; show it with a bathroom scale journey.
- Effort: M

## Why is the sky blue — and why isn't it violet?  — 20/25
- Misconception: "it reflects the ocean"; the violet question trips up most explainers.
- The number: blue scatters ~5.9× more than red; why our eyes + the solar spectrum make it blue not violet.
- Angle: the violet paradox as the twist. (Our smoke-test fixture is a 25-second sketch of this.)
- Effort: S

## How does a vaccine "teach" your immune system?  — 19/25
- Misconception: "it gives you a mild case of the disease".
- The number: memory B cells can persist for decades.
- Angle: the immune system as a library that only lends books it has read once.
- Effort: L (sensitive topic → extra review)

## How big is a mole (6×10²³) really?  — 18/25
- Misconception: big numbers are "just big".
- The number: a mole of sand grains would cover a country — compute which one, honestly.
- Angle: a scale journey where every step is a thing you've held.
- Effort: S

## How does GPS know where you are — and why does it need Einstein?  — 22/25
- Misconception: "satellites track your phone".
- The number: uncorrected relativity would drift ~10 km per day.
- Angle: your phone listens to clocks in the sky; it's a timing problem, not a tracking one.
- Effort: M
