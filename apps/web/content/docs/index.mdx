Kortix is the open-source AI Operating System. Your agents, skills, company memory and connectors live in one git repo that your company owns. Every session runs on its own cloud computer. Agents do finished work, and people review it before it lands. This guide explains Kortix in plain language, from your first chat to running a whole team on it.

## What you can do with Kortix

- **Hand off real work.** Ask an agent to research a market, draft a report, update a spreadsheet or build a small website. It works on its own computer and shows you the result.
- **Teach it how your company works.** Write down a process once as a skill. Every agent can then follow it.
- **Connect your tools.** Give agents governed access to the apps your team already uses.
- **Run work on a schedule.** Start an agent every morning, on a webhook, or when something happens in a connected app.
- **Stay in control.** Agents ask before risky actions. Their changes wait for a person to approve them.
- **Work from anywhere.** Use the web app, the desktop and mobile apps, Slack, Microsoft Teams or email.

## How it works

<Steps>
<Step title="A project holds everything">
A project is your company's workspace. Agents, skills, memory and connector settings are files in one git repo. Kortix hosts the repo for you, or you connect your own on GitHub.
</Step>
<Step title="You start a session">
A session is one piece of agent work. You type a request, and an agent picks it up. The session runs on its own cloud computer, so it never touches your laptop or other sessions.
</Step>
<Step title="The agent does the work">
The agent reads your instructions and skills, uses its tools and connectors, and produces the result. If it needs a decision, it asks you.
</Step>
<Step title="A person approves the change">
When an agent changes the project itself, for example a skill or its memory, it opens a change request. A person reviews it on the **Review** page and merges it.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Why it is built this way

- **You own it.** Your agents and know-how are files in a repo you control. Kortix is open source. Run it on Kortix Cloud, in your VPC, or on your own network.
- **Every change is reviewable.** An agent's change to the project is a change request. You see exactly what changed before it lands.
- **Credentials stay safe.** Connector credentials stay on the Kortix server. They never enter the agent's computer.
- **Everything is recorded.** The audit log records every action.

## Get started

*Create your first project, run your first chat, connect a tool, invite your team, and find your way around the web, desktop and mobile apps.*

This page takes you from your first sign-in to your first finished task. You create a project, ask an agent for something, connect one of your tools, and bring in your team. Along the way you learn where everything lives in the apps. You need no terminal and no code for any of it.

### What you need

- A Kortix sign-in on [kortix.com](https://kortix.com).
- Nothing else. Kortix can host your project's repo for you, so you need no GitHub account.

### Create your first project

A project is your company's workspace. It holds your agents, skills, memory and connector settings. A new account has no projects, and Kortix never creates one for you.

<Steps>
<Step title="Open Create a project">
Open the project menu at the top of the sidebar. Select **Switch Project** → **Create a project…**.
</Step>
<Step title="Name it">
Pick the **Account** and type a **Project name**.
</Step>
<Step title="Choose where the repo lives">
Under **Git account**, choose **Kortix managed** for the simplest start. Kortix creates and manages a private repo for you. If your company uses GitHub, pick your GitHub account instead.
</Step>
<Step title="Create it">
Click **Create project**. Your project starts with a built-in agent called `kortix` and a set of ready-made skills.
</Step>
</Steps>

### Run your first chat

<Steps>
<Step title="Start a new session">
Select **New session** in the sidebar. The project home opens with an empty message box, called the composer.
</Step>
<Step title="Ask for something real">
Type a task the way you would brief a colleague. Attach files if they help: select the attach button, paste a file, or drop it on the page.
</Step>
<Step title="Follow along">
The agent works on its own cloud computer. You see its progress in the conversation. While it works, you can send a message to steer it.
</Step>
<Step title="Review the result">
The side panel shows the agent's plan and outputs. If the agent changed files in your project, **Changes** in the header shows what changed.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="info">
The platform default model runs on every plan. You can switch models later with the **Model picker** in the composer. See [Models](/docs/#models).
</Callout>

### Connect your first tool

Agents get more useful when they can reach the apps your team uses. Connectors give them governed access. Credentials stay on the Kortix server and never enter the agent's computer.

<Steps>
<Step title="Open Connectors">
Select **Customize** in the sidebar, then the **Connectors** tab.
</Step>
<Step title="Add the app and connect an account">
Choose the app and connect your account to it.
</Step>
<Step title="Give your agent access">
Agents get no connector until you grant it. Open **Customize** → **Agents** → your agent → **Connectors**, and turn on the connector.
</Step>
</Steps>

The [Connectors](/docs/#connectors) page covers sharing accounts and approval rules.

### Invite your team

Project members are managed in the account, not in the sidebar.

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the members list">
Open **Account settings** → **Projects**, or type "members" in the command palette.
</Step>
<Step title="Invite people">
Invite your teammates to the project. They see the invite in their project list, or under **Invites** in the mobile app.
</Step>
<Step title="Let them run an agent">
A member can start sessions only with an agent assigned to them, their group, or the whole project. Assign an agent on its **People** page in **Customize** → **Agents**. You can also use **Account settings** → **Projects** → the project → **Grant access**.
</Step>
</Steps>

Roles and groups are on the [Teams](/docs/#teams) page.

### Find your way around the web app

| Where | What you find there |
| --- | --- |
| **New session** | Starts a new chat with an agent. |
| Session list | Your sessions. Group and filter them by status, owner and more. |
| **Review** | Work that waits on you, such as changes and approvals. |
| **Customize** | Every project setting: **Agents**, **Skills**, **Connectors**, **Triggers**, **Models**, **Secrets** and **Settings**. |
| **Apps** | Sites and services your agents deployed. |
| **Files** | A file browser over the project's repo. |
| Search | The command palette. Press `Cmd+K` on a Mac or `Ctrl+K` elsewhere. |
| Project menu (top left) | Switch project, **Account settings**, your personal **Settings**, and **Log out**. |

Your personal **Settings** follow you across projects: profile, security, theme, notifications and language.

### Desktop and mobile apps

- **Desktop.** Download it from [kortix.com/download](https://kortix.com/download) for macOS, Windows or Linux. It is the web app in a native window, and it updates itself. Press `Cmd+Shift+Space` (or `Ctrl+Shift+Space`) in any app to open **Quick Entry** and start a session.
- **Mobile.** Get it from the App Store or Google Play. Start and follow sessions, attach photos, dictate a message, accept invites and read your notifications.

More on each surface is on the [Everywhere](/docs/#everywhere) page.

### Examples of a first task

- "Research the top five competitors of Acme in the European market. Make a one-page comparison."
- "Turn the attached sales export into a spreadsheet with totals per region and a chart."
- "Draft a weekly status report template for the Northwind operations team."
- "Build a simple landing page for our new product and show me a preview."

### Tips

- **Brief the agent like a colleague.** Say what you want, who it is for, and what done looks like.
- **Attach the source material.** A file in the composer beats a long description.
- **Check Review often.** The **Review** count in the sidebar shows what waits on you.
- **Write it down once.** If you give the same instructions twice, turn them into a [skill](/docs/#skills).

## Agents

*What an agent is, the built-in agent every project starts with, how to create your own, what it can access, and how it asks before risky actions.*

An agent is an assistant with a job description. It has instructions that say what it does, a model it thinks with, and permissions that decide what it may touch. Every session runs one agent. You can use the built-in agent for everything, or create specialists: one for support tickets, one for sales reports, one for research. Agents matter because they turn a one-off chat into a repeatable way of working that your whole team can use.

### What an agent is made of

An agent has two halves:

- **Behavior.** Its instructions, its model, and its rules for which tools it may use freely, use after asking, or never use.
- **Access.** The connectors, secrets, skills, Apps and Kortix permissions it may use.

Access is deny-by-default. An agent gets no connector, secret or App until you grant it.

### The built-in agent

Every new project starts with an agent called `kortix`. It is the default agent: a session that names no other agent runs it. It has access to all connectors, secrets, skills and Kortix permissions in the project. You can edit its instructions to fit your company.

A new project also includes two helper agents:

- `harness-reflector` reviews recent sessions and improves the project's instructions, skills, tools and memory. It proposes every improvement as a change request and never merges it. See [Skills](/docs/#skills).
- `session-reviewer` helps it. It reviews one session and reports what went wrong.

### What you can do

- **Pick the agent for a task.** Use the **Agent picker** in the composer.
- **Create specialists.** Give each agent a narrow job and only the access that job needs.
- **Let agents work together.** A running agent can start another agent as a helper. Those sessions appear under the session that started them.
- **Decide who may run which agent.** Assign an agent to people, groups or the whole project.

### Create your own agent

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the agent list">
Select **Customize** in the sidebar, then **Agents** → **New**.
</Step>
<Step title="Choose how to create it">
**Create in chat** starts a session where an agent writes the new agent with you. Describe the job, and answer its questions. **Set up manually** opens the project files instead.
</Step>
<Step title="Set its basics and model">
Open the agent and edit its **Basics** and **Model**. The instructions say what the agent does, how, and what it must never do.
</Step>
<Step title="Give it access">
Under **Access**, turn on what the job needs: **Skills**, **Connectors**, **Secrets**, **Apps** and **Kortix permissions**.
</Step>
<Step title="Assign it to people">
On the agent's **People** page, choose who may run it.
</Step>
</Steps>

A change to an agent takes effect when it lands in the project. New sessions start with it, and running sessions pick it up.

### What an agent can access

| Access | What it allows |
| --- | --- |
| **Connectors** | Calls to the apps you connected, such as your help desk or CRM. |
| **Secrets** | Encrypted credentials, such as an API key. |
| **Skills** | The playbooks the agent may load. |
| **Apps** | The project's Apps whose access is restricted or private. |
| **Kortix permissions** | Actions in Kortix itself, such as starting sessions or merging changes. |
| **Tools** | Built-in abilities such as web search, image search, reading web pages and project memory. |

**An agent acts as itself, not as you.** Kortix gives each agent its own identity in each project. The agent does the same thing whoever runs it. Your own role does not lend it extra power.

**Your personal accounts stay personal.** A personal connector account, personal secret or your own computer reaches a session only when the session is private and runs on your behalf. A shared session or a scheduled run reaches none of them.

The **What this agent can do** panel on the agent's **Kortix permissions** page shows exactly what the agent may do.

### How an agent asks before risky actions

You decide what an agent does without a person. Each of its tools follows one of three rules:

| Rule | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Allow | The tool runs. |
| Ask | The agent waits until a person approves or rejects the action. |
| Deny | The agent cannot use the tool. |

Approvals also come from connectors. A connector rule can hold an action, such as sending an email, until a person approves it. You see these under **Approvals** in the session header and on the **Review** page. Approve or deny each one.

Changes to the project itself go through review too. When an agent changes a skill, its memory or another agent, it opens a change request. A person reviews it on the **Review** page and selects **Ship it** to merge it.

<Callout type="warning">
The built-in `kortix` agent is allowed to merge its own change requests. Its standing instructions tell it to stop after it opens one, so a person merges. To require a person every time, remove the merge permission from the agent's **Kortix permissions**.
</Callout>

### Examples

- **Support triage at Northwind.** A `support` agent reads each new ticket, checks the order history, drafts a reply and submits it for review. Its instructions say: never close a ticket yourself.
- **Weekly sales report at Acme.** A `sales-reporter` agent has access to the CRM connector only. A schedule starts it every Monday, and it posts a summary for the team.
- **Market research.** A `researcher` agent may search the web and read pages, but has no access to company apps.
- **Contract review.** A `legal-review` agent reads the documents you attach and flags clauses. It has no connectors and no secrets.

### Tips

- **One job per agent.** A narrow agent is easier to trust and easier to improve.
- **Grant the least access that works.** Start with nothing and add what the job needs.
- **Use Ask for anything you would want to double-check.** Switch to Allow once you trust the agent.
- **Put repeatable steps in skills, not instructions.** Several agents can then share them. See [Skills](/docs/#skills).
- **Write a clear description.** Other agents read it to decide which helper to pick.

## Skills

*A skill is a written playbook an agent follows for a specific task: when to write one, how to add one, and how agents improve their own skills with your approval.*

A skill is a written playbook. It tells an agent how your company does one specific task, step by step. An agent loads a skill only when the task needs it, so you can have many skills without cluttering every conversation. Skills matter because they capture know-how once. Every agent you allow can then follow the same process, and the process gets better over time instead of living in one person's head.

### What a skill is

- **A short document in your project.** It has a name, a description, and the steps. Reference files, such as a template or a checklist, can sit beside it.
- **Loaded on demand.** The agent reads each skill's description and decides when to load it. Write the description as "when to use this", not only "what this is".
- **Shared.** One skill can serve many agents. You choose which agents may load it.
- **Versioned.** Skills are files in your project's git repo. Every change is recorded, and you can see who changed what.

### When to write a skill

Write a skill when:

- You give an agent the same instructions for the second time.
- A task has a fixed order of steps, a required format, or rules that must not be skipped.
- Several agents or several people need to do the task the same way.
- A new teammate would need a written guide to do the task well.

Keep a one-off request in the chat. Keep an agent's general role in its instructions. A skill is for one repeatable task.

### Skills you get out of the box

A new project includes ready-made skills for common work: documents, spreadsheets, PDFs, presentations, websites, web apps, browsing the web and converting documents to Markdown.

Kortix also adds a set of system skills to every session. They teach agents how to use Kortix itself, for example its memory, connectors and Apps. You cannot change or remove these.

More skills are in the [marketplace](https://kortix.com/marketplace). Installing one starts a session that adds the skill to your project and opens a change request for you to review.

### Add a skill

<Steps>
<Step title="Open Skills">
Select **Customize** in the sidebar, then **Skills** → **New**.
</Step>
<Step title="Choose how to write it">
**Create in chat** starts a session where an agent writes the skill with you. Describe the task and how your team does it. **Create with a form** lets you write it yourself.
</Step>
<Step title="Give agents access">
Open **Customize** → **Agents** → the agent → **Skills**, and turn on the new skill. The built-in `kortix` agent may load every skill.
</Step>
<Step title="Try it">
Start a session and ask for the task. The agent loads the skill when the task matches its description.
</Step>
</Steps>

To remove a skill, open its card under **Customize** → **Skills** and select **Remove**.

### Agents improve their own skills

Skills do not have to stay as you first wrote them. Agents can propose improvements, and a person decides what lands.

<Steps>
<Step title="An agent proposes a change">
An agent edits a skill in its own session. The edit does not affect anyone else yet.
</Step>
<Step title="It opens a change request">
The agent opens a change request. It shows exactly which lines of the skill changed.
</Step>
<Step title="A person reviews it">
The change appears on the **Review** page. Select **Ship it** to merge it, or ask for changes. Your note goes back to the agent that proposed it.
</Step>
<Step title="Every agent uses the new version">
Once merged, new sessions use the improved skill. Running sessions pick it up as well.
</Step>
</Steps>

Every new project includes the `harness-reflector` agent. It reviews recent sessions, then improves skills, instructions, tools and memory. It bundles its work into one change request titled `harness: …` and never merges it. A daily schedule runs it, but the schedule ships switched off. Turning it on is a one-line change to the project config, merged like any other change.

<Callout type="info">
No agent change to a skill reaches your team until a person approves it. You stay in control of how your company works.
</Callout>

### Examples

- **Weekly sales report at Acme.** The skill names the CRM fields to pull, the regions to group by, the chart to include, and who receives the report.
- **Support ticket triage at Northwind.** The skill says how to classify a ticket by product area and urgency, and which tickets go to a person right away.
- **New customer onboarding.** The skill lists the welcome email template, the accounts to create, and the checklist to send to the account manager.
- **Brand-safe writing.** The skill holds the tone of voice, the words to avoid, and a template for blog posts.

### Tips

- **Write the description first.** It decides when an agent loads the skill. Start with "Use when…".
- **Use numbered steps.** Agents follow an ordered list more reliably than prose.
- **Add examples of good output.** Put a sample report or reply in a reference file beside the skill.
- **Keep each skill to one task.** Split a long skill into two.
- **Read the change requests.** An agent's proposed edit often shows where your process was unclear.

## Memory and Drive

*How your agents remember what your company knows, and how people and agents share files in Kortix Drive.*

Agents do better work when they know your company. Kortix gives them two places to find that knowledge. **Memory** is a set of files in your project that describes your company: its purpose, its connections, its conventions and its decisions. **Kortix Drive** (the Files feature) is a set of shared folders where people and agents keep documents side by side. Memory tells an agent how you work. Drive holds the material it works on.

### What you can do

- Write down what every agent must know once, and stop repeating it in each chat.
- Let agents add to memory as they learn, and review each change before it counts.
- Give every person a private folder that their own sessions can open.
- Share a folder with people, groups or agents, so everyone works from the same files.
- Get back the files an agent made, even after its computer has gone to sleep.

### Company memory

Memory lives in the `memory/` folder of your project. One file, `MEMORY.md`, is the index. It has one line for each memory file.

#### How agents use it

- **Reading.** Kortix does not paste memory into every prompt. A built-in skill tells each agent to read the index at the start of a task, then open the files the index points to.
- **Writing.** Agents create and edit memory files as they work. They can view, create, edit, rename and delete files in `memory/` only.
- **Reviewing.** A memory edit is an ordinary change on the session's own branch. Other sessions see it only after someone merges it into your project. You stay in control of what your company "knows".
- **Cleaning up.** Every new project includes a reflection agent. When you turn on its daily schedule, it reviews recent sessions and tidies memory, skills and prompts. It opens a change for you to review and never merges on its own.

<Callout type="warning">
Never put passwords, API keys or personal data in memory. Store credentials as project secrets instead.
</Callout>

### Kortix Drive

Drive is the Files feature: shared folders for the whole project, kept in sync with your sessions.

#### How folders work

- **Your own folder.** Every person has a private folder. In a private session you started, it opens as `/drives/me`.
- **Shared folders.** Any folder can be shared with people, groups and agents.
- **What a session sees.** A new session opens the folders that its agent, or the whole project, has access to. In a private session that you started, it also opens your own folder and every folder shared with you or your groups.
- **Where to manage them.** Open **Customize** → **Files**.

#### Turn it on

Drive needs the Platinum computer provider and the project's **Volumes** flag.

<Steps>
<Step title="Open feature flags">
In your project, open **Customize** → **Settings** → **Feature flags**.
</Step>
<Step title="Turn on Volumes">
Turn on **Volumes**. The Files feature follows this flag, so you turn on one switch, not two.
</Step>
<Step title="Start a new session">
The folders appear in sessions that start after the change. Sessions already running keep what they had.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="info">
With Files on, a session on a provider other than Platinum does not start. Volumes is an experimental flag.
</Callout>

### Where else your files live

Drive is one of four places a session keeps files. You rarely need the other three, but it helps to know them.

| Place | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| The session's computer | Work in progress on the agent's own cloud computer. |
| Your project's repository | What the agent committed. Open it from the project's **Files** page. |
| Session attachments | Files people attached, and copies of files the agent showed you. Kortix saves them at the end of every turn, so you can download them while the session sleeps. |
| Shared folders (Drive) | Folders for people and agents, managed in **Customize** → **Files**. |

When you delete a session, Kortix keeps its conversation and its stored files for 30 days, so you can restore it. Read more in [Agents](/docs/#agents) and [Computers](/docs/#computers).

### Examples

- **Acme's brand rules.** Acme writes its tone of voice and product names into one memory file. Every agent reads it before it drafts an email or a page.
- **Northwind's finance folder.** Northwind shares a "Finance" folder with its finance group and its bookkeeping agent. Invoices dropped there are ready for the agent in its next session.
- **A personal research pile.** You keep reports in your own folder. Your private sessions can open them. Your colleagues' sessions cannot.
- **Decisions that stick.** An agent learns that Acme bills in euros and records it in memory. After you merge the change, every later session knows it.

### Tips

- Keep `MEMORY.md` short. One clear line per file helps agents find the right file fast.
- Review memory changes like any other change. A wrong fact in memory spreads to every session.
- Share folders with the agent that needs them, not with the whole project, when the files are sensitive.
- Use Drive for documents people also edit. Use memory for short facts about how your company works.
- Goals keep their working files in Drive. See [Outcomes](/docs/#outcomes).

## Connectors

*Connect the tools your company already uses, decide who may use each connection, and choose which actions run, ask first or are blocked.*

A connector gives your agents safe access to one outside app, such as Gmail, Linear, Notion or Stripe. Agents use it as a tool, so they can read your inbox, update a ticket or look up a customer. Kortix keeps the login, picks the right account, applies your rules and records every call. The login itself never enters the agent's computer. You decide what each agent may do, and nothing outside those rules happens.

### What you can do

- Connect an app with a sign-in, often in one click.
- Choose who may use a connection: only you, the whole project, or specific people, groups or agents.
- Set a rule for each action: **Allow**, **Ask** or **Block**.
- Give one agent stricter rules than another on the same app.
- Approve or deny a held action from Kortix, Slack or Teams.
- Use Kortix from Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor and other AI tools.

### How it works

Three ideas answer three questions:

| Idea | Question it answers | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Connector** | How does Kortix reach the app? | Linear, through its own server |
| **Account** | Who does a call run as? | The team inbox, or your own inbox |
| **Rule set** | What may this agent do? | Read only, for the support agent |

An agent can use a connector only when the agent has been given access to it. The person or agent must also be allowed to use the account.

#### Who can use an account

- **Only you.** A private account. Only you can use it, and only in your own private sessions. This is the default.
- **Everyone in the project.** A shared account that every member and every allowed agent can use.
- **Specific people, groups, or agents.** A shared account for a chosen audience.

Automations never use a private account. They need an account shared with the whole project. See [Automations](/docs/#automations).

### Set it up

<Steps>
<Step title="Find the app">
Open **Customize** → **Connectors**. On the **All** tab, find the app. **One-click only** is on by default and lists the apps you connect by signing in.
</Step>
<Step title="Install it">
Select **Install**. Choose **Who can use it**. **Only you** is selected by default.
</Step>
<Step title="Sign in">
Sign in to the app, or enter its API key. You return to the connector's page with its tools loaded.
</Step>
<Step title="Check the rules">
Open the connector's **Tools** tab. It shows each action and whether it runs, needs approval, or is blocked.
</Step>
</Steps>

An app that is not in the catalog can still connect. Select **New** → **MCP server** and paste its address, or **New** → **Add a custom connector** for another kind of API. **New** → **Create in chat** lets an agent set it up and open a change for you to review.

If an agent needs an app that nobody has connected yet, the chat shows a **Connect** button. It opens the same sign-in, as a private account by default.

### Allow, Ask, Block

Every action follows one of three rules:

- **Allow** runs the action at once.
- **Ask** holds the action until a person approves it.
- **Block** never runs it.

You can also mark a whole connector as sensitive. Then every action without its own rule needs approval, reads included. A project can use a careful default too: reads run, and writes or deletions wait for approval.

#### Approving a held action

<Steps>
<Step title="Find it">
Open **Review** → **Needs you**, follow the approval link, or use the approval card in a Slack or Teams thread. The session shows **Needs you** in your lists.
</Step>
<Step title="Check it">
The page shows what the app will receive, with private values hidden.
</Step>
<Step title="Decide">
Choose **Approve** or **Deny**, with an optional note to the agent. The agent then continues.
</Step>
</Steps>

- An approval covers one exact action. A changed detail needs a new approval.
- A held action expires when nobody decides within 24 hours, unless your project sets another time.
- A project manager, or the person who started the session, decides.
- There is no "approve everything for this session". An action that must run unattended needs an **Allow** rule.

### Use Kortix from Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor

Kortix also works in the other direction. Your AI tool can start sessions, read results and call your connectors through Kortix. It acts as you, with your permissions. You install nothing.

<Steps>
<Step title="Copy the address">
In the web app, open the project menu (top left) and select **Connect MCP**. The dialog shows the address and an install action for Claude, Claude Code, Cursor, Codex and other tools.
</Step>
<Step title="Add it to your tool">
In Claude, open **Settings** → **Connectors** → **Add custom connector** and paste the address. ChatGPT needs developer mode and **OAuth** sign-in. In Cursor, select **Add** on the Cursor row.
</Step>
<Step title="Sign in">
Your browser opens the Kortix consent screen. Approve the app only when you connected it yourself.
</Step>
</Steps>

To remove access, open **Settings** → **Personal access keys** → **Connected apps** and select **Revoke access**.

### Examples

- **Acme support.** Acme's support agent reads the shared support inbox but cannot send. Its sales agent uses the same inbox and sends to customers after an approval.
- **Northwind finance.** Northwind marks its Stripe connector sensitive. Every action an agent takes there waits for a finance lead.
- **Your own calendar.** You connect Google Calendar for **Only you**. Your private sessions can plan your week. Nobody else's sessions can see it.
- **Kortix from Claude.** A team lead asks Claude to start a Kortix session that drafts the weekly report, then reads the result in Claude.

### Tips

- Connect each account once. Give agents different rule sets instead of adding the same app twice.
- Start strict. Use **Ask** for anything that sends, pays or deletes, and loosen rules once you trust the pattern.
- Share accounts with the agent that needs them, not with everyone, when the data is sensitive.
- Every call is recorded with the account it ran as, so you can always see who did what.

## Automations

*Run agents around the clock on a schedule, when another system calls Kortix, or when something happens in a connected app.*

An automation starts agent work with nobody at the keyboard. In Kortix, an automation is a **trigger**. A trigger has two parts: what starts it, and what the agent should do. A schedule can run your morning summary every weekday. A new email can start triage before anyone opens the inbox. Each run gets its own session on its own cloud computer, so your team finds the work done, not waiting.

### What you can do

- Run an agent on a schedule: daily, weekly, every few minutes, or once at a set time.
- Start an agent when another system calls Kortix (a webhook).
- Start an agent when something happens in a connected app, such as a new email, a new calendar event or a new file.
- Ask an agent to check back on its own work later with a reminder.
- See every run, fire one by hand, and pause all automations at once.

### The kinds of trigger

| Trigger | Starts when | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Schedule** | A time arrives, or a fixed period passes. | Daily reports, weekly reviews, regular checks. |
| **Webhook** | Another system sends a signed request to the trigger's address. | Form tools, payment systems, code hosts. |
| **Connector event** | Something new appears in a connected app. | A new email, a new calendar event, a new file. |
| **App event** | One of your own Kortix Apps reports something. | Sources that fit none of the above. See [Apps](/docs/#apps). |

To react to something outside Kortix, pick the first that fits: a connector event, then a webhook, then a schedule.

### What happens when a trigger fires

1. Kortix fills in your instruction with the details of the event, such as the email's subject.
2. It starts a new session by default, with your chosen agent and model, on its own computer.
3. The agent does the work, using the connectors it may use.
4. The **Triggers** page shows the trigger's last run and its status. The session appears in your lists.

A few rules keep this safe:

- **Outside content is marked.** When an event comes from another app, the session's first line says it is automated, third-party content, so the agent treats it as data, not as orders.
- **Shared accounts only.** A connector event reads from an account shared with the whole project. A private account never feeds a trigger.
- **Nobody to ask.** If an automated session waits on a person, it waits at most 30 minutes, then stops. Use connector rules that let the needed actions run. See [Connectors](/docs/#connectors).
- **Who can see the runs.** By default, only people who manage triggers, plus account owners and admins, can open the sessions. You can choose **Selected teammates** or **Whole project** instead.
- **One run at a time.** A schedule skips a run while its previous run is still going.

### Set it up

<Steps>
<Step title="Open Triggers">
Open your project, then **Triggers**. The tabs are **All**, **Schedules**, **Connector events** and **Webhooks**.
</Step>
<Step title="Create the trigger">
Select **New trigger**, then **Set up manually**. Under **When**, choose a schedule, a connector event or a webhook.
</Step>
<Step title="Pick the account">
For a connector event, under **Connection**, pick a connector and one of its shared accounts, or connect one.
</Step>
<Step title="Write the instruction">
Under **Then**, write what the agent should do, and pick the agent and the model.
</Step>
<Step title="Create and test">
Choose **Create trigger**. A connector event works when its badge reads **Live**. Select **Run now** to try it once.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="info">
Connector events need the project flag **Composio event triggers**. It is off by default. Turn it on in **Customize** → **Settings** → **Feature flags**. A webhook also needs a signing secret, stored as a project secret.
</Callout>

### Reminders

A reminder is different from a trigger. It does not start a new session. It sends a message into one session that already exists, later, once or on repeat. For example: "in 24 hours, check whether the vendor replied." The session wakes with its full history and continues. Reminders are on by default. Your project's **Reminders** page lists them.

### Keeping automations under control

- **Run limits.** A trigger starts at most 60 runs an hour unless you set another limit under **Options** → **Run limits**. You can add a daily limit too.
- **Auto-pause.** A trigger that hits its hourly limit for 2 hours in a row pauses itself. It shows **Paused** until you select **Resume**.
- **Failures.** A failed run shows **Last run didn't finish**. With the project's notification center on, the people who set up the trigger get one alert when it starts failing and one when it recovers.
- **Pause everything.** **Account settings** → **Settings** → **Automations** → **Pause all automations** stops every schedule, webhook, event and reminder in every project.

### Examples

- **Acme morning brief.** Every weekday at 9:00 Berlin time, an agent summarizes yesterday's sales activity and posts it for the team.
- **Northwind support inbox.** Each new email to the support address starts an agent that sorts it and drafts a reply for approval.
- **New leads.** Acme's website form sends each lead to a webhook. An agent adds it to the CRM and books a follow-up.
- **Follow-up.** An agent sends a quote, then sets a reminder to check for a reply in two days.

### Tips

- Write the instruction as you would brief a colleague: the goal, the steps and where the result goes.
- Test with **Run now** before you rely on a schedule.
- Give automated agents **Allow** rules for the actions they need. Nobody is there to approve an **Ask**.
- Every run costs model time. Set run limits on triggers that fire often.
- For work that needs many days and real follow-through, use an [outcome](/docs/#outcomes) instead.

## Outcomes

*Give Kortix an outcome, and an agent owns it until it is verifiably done: it plans, works, chases people, waits and has the result checked.*

Most requests are tasks: do this once, now. Some are outcomes: "make sure our old bank account is fully closed", or "get the grant application submitted". An outcome can take days or months and depends on other people. In Kortix, an outcome is a **Goal**. You state the outcome once and walk away. An agent plans the work, does it, chases the people it needs, waits when it must, and stops only when a separate check confirms the result.

### What you can do

- Hand over an objective, not a list of steps.
- Agree up front on how "done" will be proven.
- Let the agent follow up with you and with others until it has what it needs.
- Watch progress at any time, and edit the goal's files yourself.
- Stop a goal whenever you choose.

### How it works

#### The goal folder

Each goal is a folder in Kortix Drive, in your own folder or in a shared company folder. The folder is the goal's memory. If a session ends, a new one continues from the folder alone. It holds:

- the goal file: the objective, its success criteria, the open items and the status;
- a log, with one line per action;
- notes: your original request, the constraints and one thread per person;
- `docs/` for material, and `evidence/` for proof.

You can open and edit these files. Your edits win. See [Memory and Drive](/docs/#memory-and-drive).

#### Plan

The agent writes 2 to 6 success criteria that someone else can check. Each one names its proof. It records your constraints, such as a spending limit. Anything outside a constraint becomes a question for you, not an action.

#### Work

The agent acts on the most useful open item on each turn. It can hand heavy or parallel work to worker sessions, each doing one item.

#### Chase

The agent depends on people, so it acts like a colleague who needs something to do the job.

- It asks once, precisely: what it needs, why, and how to send it.
- It uses the channels your project already has, such as Slack. It keeps one thread per person and asks you to reply there.
- It follows up when an answer is late, and each follow-up is firmer: a reminder, then the cost of the delay, then a short daily ping.
- It chases outside parties, such as a vendor or a bank, the same way.

#### Wake

Nothing runs between turns. Three things wake the goal: a daily heartbeat reminder, a reply from a person, or a step the agent scheduled for itself. If the session's computer fails, the next heartbeat starts a new session that takes over.

#### Check

The agent never judges its own work. When every criterion has its proof, it starts a separate verifier session that did none of the work. The verifier checks each proof and writes PASS or FAIL. The goal is done only when everything passes. A FAIL sends the goal back to work.

A goal moves through these states: draft, active, waiting on a person, waiting on an outside event, verifying, then done. It ends early only when you say stop. It never ends because it is hard, slow or blocked. A blocked goal waits and chases.

### Set it up

<Steps>
<Step title="Turn on the features">
Goals is an experimental feature that needs Platinum. In **Customize** → **Settings** → **Feature flags**, turn on **Goals**, **Volumes** and **Reminders**.
</Step>
<Step title="State the outcome">
In a chat, describe the outcome, for example "make sure…" or "keep at it until…". Say if it belongs to your team or company. Otherwise it goes in your own folder.
</Step>
<Step title="Read the plan">
The agent replies with the outcome, the success criteria, the first thing it needs from you, and where to watch it.
</Step>
<Step title="Watch it">
Open the **Goals** page in the project sidebar for status, health, the next wake and what the goal waits on. In the session, the **Goal** button in the header shows the same.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning">
Do not delete the session that coordinates an open goal. If you delete it, the goal stops waking.
</Callout>

### Examples

- **Close an account.** Acme asks: "make sure our old business bank account is fully closed." The agent requests the closure, asks the finance lead to approve a sign-in prompt, waits for written confirmation, and checks for charges afterwards.
- **Submit an application.** Northwind needs a funding application in before a deadline. The agent drafts it, chases each colleague for their figures, and raises the pressure as the deadline nears.
- **Collect documents.** An agent gathers signed forms from five suppliers. It keeps one thread per supplier and marks each form received in the goal file.
- **Fix a recurring problem.** Acme wants failed invoice emails to stop. The agent finds the cause, fixes it, and a verifier confirms that new invoices arrive.

### Tips

- Phrase the goal as a result that is true or false: "the account is closed", not "work on the account".
- Name hard limits up front, such as budget or who may be contacted.
- Answer in the thread the agent opened. That reply wakes the goal at once.
- Keep passwords out of the goal folder. Store them as secrets.
- For a simple recurring job, a [schedule](/docs/#automations) is enough. Use a goal when the work needs follow-through.

## Computers

*Every session runs on its own cloud computer, and you can connect your own computer so agents can use its files, shell and screen under rules you set.*

Agents need a place to do real work. In Kortix, every session gets its own cloud computer: its own isolated machine with your project's files, a terminal, a browser and the usual office tools. The agent runs code, reads and writes files, builds documents and starts apps there, without touching anyone else's work. When an agent needs something that lives on your own laptop or desktop, you can connect that computer too, and you decide on the machine itself what agents may do on it.

### What you can do

- **Give every task its own computer.** Each [session](/docs/#get-started) starts on a fresh cloud computer with a copy of your project. Work in one session never mixes with another.
- **Let the agent run code and use tools.** The computer has Python, Node.js, a built-in browser, and document tools such as LibreOffice, Pandoc and text recognition (OCR) for scanned pages.
- **Open what the agent builds.** When the agent starts a website or dashboard on its computer, you open it from the session's preview panel.
- **Get files in and out.** Attach files to a session. The files people attach, and the files the agent shows you, are saved with the session.
- **Run several sessions at once.** Each session has its own computer, so you can hand out several tasks side by side. An agent can also split a large task into child sessions and collect their results.
- **Connect your own computer.** Let agents read and write files on your machine, run programs there or, with Computer Use, see the screen and use its apps.

### How the cloud computer works

<Steps>
<Step title="You start a session">
Kortix gives the session its own branch of your project and boots a cloud computer for it.
</Step>
<Step title="The agent works on its computer">
The agent reads your project, runs commands, browses and writes files. It saves its work to the session's own branch.
</Step>
<Step title="A person approves the change">
When the agent changes the project, it opens a change request. A person reviews it and merges it.
</Step>
<Step title="The computer sleeps when idle">
About 15 to 20 minutes after the last turn, Kortix stops the computer. It keeps the disk, the branch and the conversation. A new message wakes it.
</Step>
</Steps>

A stopped computer costs nothing while it sleeps. A preview request or a new prompt wakes it again.

#### Previews

Any website or tool that the agent starts on its computer is reachable from outside it. The session page's preview panel offers common ports, such as **App preview** and **Frontend preview**, plus any other port. A preview asks you to sign in to Kortix first. To show it to someone outside Kortix, the session owner creates a public link.

#### When you delete a session

A deleted session stays restorable for 30 days. Open **Recently deleted** on the Sessions page and select **Restore session**. Only work that the agent saved to its branch survives: unsaved files on the computer are gone.

<Callout type="info">
Your team can change what is installed on the cloud computer and how large it is. A developer sets this once for the whole project in the [kortix.yaml reference](/docs/project/manifest).
</Callout>

### Connect your own computer

Some work needs your own machine: a file on your desktop, a program only you have installed, or an app on your screen. A connected computer is your real machine. Agents act on it as your user.

When you connect it, you choose up to three capabilities:

| Capability | What agents can do |
| --- | --- |
| **Filesystem** | Read, write, list and delete files. |
| **Shell** | Run programs. |
| **Computer Use** | See the screen and use the apps. |

Your computer is private by default. Only you use it, from your private sessions, in every project you belong to.

#### Set it up

<Steps>
<Step title="Start the connection">
In the Kortix desktop app, select **Connect this computer** in the sidebar. A browser window opens the approval page.
</Step>
<Step title="Check the code and name the computer">
Check that the code on the page matches the code on the computer. Give the computer a name, for example "Work laptop".
</Step>
<Step title="Choose who can use it">
Keep **Only you**, or choose **Also share with** the current project.
</Step>
<Step title="Choose the capabilities and approve">
Select **Filesystem**, **Shell**, **Computer Use**, or a mix. On macOS, select **Allow all** when asked, and give Kortix the permissions macOS requests.
</Step>
</Steps>

The computer stays connected after you quit the app.

#### You stay in control on the machine

The computer itself decides whether an agent may use it, whatever the project allows. Its **Access** setting has three modes:

- **Ask each time.** The first call shows a prompt on your computer: **Allow for 1 hour**, **Allow for 24 hours** or **Deny**. This is the default when the desktop app runs.
- **Always allowed.** Every call runs without a prompt.
- **Off.** Every call is refused.

Only you, at the computer, can widen access. Narrowing it works from anywhere and applies at once. You can also pause the connection, or turn on **Keep this computer awake** while it is plugged in.

#### Share it with your team

Open **Customize → Connectors → Computers → Accounts** and select **Share** on your computer's row. Pick people, groups or the whole project. A computer shared with everyone also serves automations that run with nobody present. Your **Access** setting still applies: in **Ask each time**, every approval is yours to give.

### Examples

- **Acme's finance lead** asks an agent to turn last month's exports into a board report. The agent works on its cloud computer, builds the charts and the PDF, and attaches the file to the session.
- **Northwind's product team** asks for a prototype of a customer portal. The agent starts it on its computer, and the team clicks through it in the preview panel before anything ships.
- **An operations manager at Acme** connects a work laptop with **Filesystem** only. An agent then reads the spreadsheets in a local folder and writes a summary next to them.
- **Northwind's support lead** runs three sessions at once: one drafts replies, one cleans a contact list, one researches a vendor. Each works on its own computer.

### Tips

- Keep **Ask each time** on your own computer until you trust a workflow. Approve for 1 hour, not 24.
- Choose only the capabilities the work needs. **Shell** lets an agent run any program your user can run.
- To hold risky actions for a person, set an **Ask** or **Block** policy on the **Computers** connector. See [Connectors](/docs/#connectors).
- To keep a computer connected but unused, set its access to **Off**.
- For how Kortix protects your work and data, see [Security](/docs/#security).

## Apps

*Build and host websites, internal tools and backends at a stable URL, with Kortix sign-in, access control and releases built in.*

An App is a website, an internal tool or a backend that your project hosts on Kortix. Each App has one stable URL, a setting for who may open it, and numbered releases. Kortix signs people in before your App runs, so a tool for your team needs no login system of its own. You do not need to be a developer to get one: ask an agent to build and deploy it, then share the link.

### What you can do

- **Host a website or a page.** A static App is a set of files that Kortix serves directly, with no server to run.
- **Run an internal tool.** A server App runs your code on machines that Kortix builds and keeps running.
- **Keep data.** A backend App can hold a database with server functions, realtime updates, file storage, schedules and search, from a ready-made template.
- **Decide who opens it.** Keep an App private, open it to the project, to chosen people and groups, to anyone with a password, or to the public.
- **Know who is looking.** Kortix tells your App who the signed-in visitor is: name, email, role and groups.
- **Ship safely.** Every deploy makes a new release. A failed release never replaces the live one, and you can roll back.
- **Connect Apps to agents.** An App can ask an agent to start work, and an automation can call an App on a schedule.
- **List tools you already use.** Add a dashboard or vendor tool that runs elsewhere, and Kortix shows it beside your own Apps.

### How it works

Every App you may open is listed at **Apps** in the project sidebar. The page shows each App's live state, a preview and its access controls. An App's detail lists its releases.

You create and deploy Apps from an agent session, the command line or code. For most people, that means you describe the App to an agent. The agent builds it on its [cloud computer](/docs/#computers) and deploys it.

#### Who can open an App

| Access | Who can open the App |
| --- | --- |
| Private (the default) | Only the person who created it |
| Project | Everyone who can read the project |
| Restricted | The members and groups you list |
| Password | Anyone with the password |
| Public | Anyone. No sign-in. |

Project managers, account owners and account admins can open every App in the project. A private App stays manageable after its creator leaves. When you change who can access an App, everyone signs in again.

#### Always on or on demand

A server App runs in one of two modes:

- **Always on.** It runs around the clock, and Kortix restarts it when it stops. Use it for live updates, background work and databases.
- **On demand.** It stops after a quiet period. The next visitor or automation wakes it. A stopped on-demand App uses no compute.

A new App is always on. A static App has nothing to run, so neither mode applies.

#### Releases and rollback

Each deploy creates a new numbered release. A release never changes. If a new release fails its checks, visitors keep the previous one. A rollback creates a new release from an older one. An App's stored data stays when you roll back, because a rollback changes code and settings, never the data. Kortix also takes a daily snapshot of an App's stored data.

### Set it up

<Steps>
<Step title="Describe the App to an agent">
Start a session and say what you need: who uses it, what it shows, and what data it keeps.
</Step>
<Step title="Let the agent build and deploy it">
The agent builds the App on its cloud computer and deploys it. A new App is private: only you can open it.
</Step>
<Step title="Open it and check it">
Open the App from **Apps** in the project sidebar, in a browser where you are signed in to Kortix.
</Step>
<Step title="Share it">
Set who may open it, for example the whole project or a group. Then send people the link.
</Step>
</Steps>

To add a tool that runs somewhere else, select **Add app** on the Apps page and enter its address.

### Examples

- **An internal sales dashboard at Acme.** An agent builds a dashboard that reads the team's numbers and shares it with the Sales group. Each person sees it under their own Kortix login.
- **A customer request form at Northwind.** A public page lets customers send a request. The App posts an event for each new request, and an [automation](/docs/#automations) hands it to an agent that drafts a reply.
- **A small database-backed tool at Acme.** Operations tracks equipment loans in an App with its own database. The tool knows who is signed in, so it records who took what.
- **A vendor tool in one place.** Northwind adds its analytics dashboard, which runs elsewhere, so the team opens it from the same Apps page.

### Tips

- Start private. Open the App to the project or a group once it works.
- Never use **Public** for internal data. A public App does not know who the visitor is.
- Choose on demand for a tool people open now and then. Choose always on for anything that must answer at once or keep running.
- Running Apps use compute from your account's credits. When the balance runs out, Kortix stops the account's running Apps until credits are added. See [Billing](/docs/#billing).
- An agent opens a private or restricted App only when that App is on the agent's list of Apps. See [Agents](/docs/#agents).

**For developers:** [Apps reference](/docs/apps) · [CLI](/docs/cli)


## Channels

*Work with agents from Slack, Microsoft Teams and email, where each person acts as their own linked Kortix account.*

Your team already lives in Slack, Microsoft Teams and email. Channels bring agents there. Mention the bot with a task, and the agent replies in the same thread with live progress. In Slack and Teams, each person links their own Kortix account once. After that, Kortix knows who asked, checks that this person may run the agent, and keeps each project's work inside that project's own conversations.

### What you can do

- **Hand off a task where you talk.** Mention the bot in a Slack channel or Teams chat, or message it directly.
- **Follow the work live.** The agent replies in the thread with its steps while it works, then the answer.
- **Answer questions and approve actions in place.** When the agent needs a decision or an approval, a card with buttons appears in the thread.
- **Stop a run.** The agent's live message has a **Stop** button.
- **Pick the agent and model per channel.** Each channel can run its own agent and model.
- **Email the agent.** Each email thread becomes one session, and the agent replies by email.

### How it works

#### One thread, one session

- **In Slack, one thread is one session.** The first message in a thread starts the session. Later messages in the thread continue it.
- **In a Slack channel, only a mention reaches the agent.** A reply without a mention stays between people. The agent reads the thread the next time someone mentions it.
- **In a direct message with the bot, every message reaches the agent.** That session is private to you.
- **In Teams, each channel post is its own session.** A personal or group chat keeps one session until someone sends `/new`.

#### Each person is their own account

A Slack or Teams message carries no Kortix login. So each person links their Kortix account once, with `/kortix login` in Slack or `/login` in Teams. Only you see the link. It works once and expires after 10 minutes.

After that:

- Kortix checks that the sender is a project member who may start sessions.
- The agent acts with its own permissions, not with the sender's role.
- Your own keys and connected accounts reach only your private chat with the bot, never a shared channel.
- A person who is not in the project gets a card to request access. The account owners and admins receive the request.

#### Who may join a conversation

Each channel has a join policy. It decides who may join a session that started there.

| Policy | Who may join |
| --- | --- |
| Open (the default) | Any linked project member |
| Approval | The session owner approves or denies each new person |
| Owner only | Only the person who started the session |

#### Approvals in the thread

When an agent asks for a decision, the card shows the title, a summary, a risk note and buttons, such as **Approve** and **Deny**. Every card also has **View in Kortix**. When an agent wants to use a connected tool that needs approval, the card shows what it will do. A project manager, or the person who started the session, selects **Approve** or **Deny**.

### Set it up

<Steps>
<Step title="Connect Slack or Teams">
Open **Customize → Connectors** and select the **Channels** scope. Select **Add to Slack**, or connect **Microsoft Teams**. For Teams, a tenant admin grants consent once, and the Kortix app appears in your organization's Teams catalog.
</Step>
<Step title="Add the bot">
Invite the bot to a Slack channel, or add the Kortix app to a Teams chat or channel. A direct message with the bot needs no invite.
</Step>
<Step title="Link your account">
Send `/kortix login` in Slack or `/login` in Teams, open the link, and sign in. You do this once per Slack workspace or Teams tenant.
</Step>
<Step title="Mention the bot with a task">
The agent replies in the thread, with live progress.
</Step>
</Steps>

When one Slack workspace or Teams tenant serves several projects, the first message in a new channel shows a project picker. The choice binds the channel to that project.

#### Email

Email is experimental and off by default. Turn on the **Email channel** project flag in **Customize → Settings → Feature flags**. Then open **Customize → Connectors → Kortix** and select **Connect** on Email. The inbox runs on your own Resend account, or on a Kortix-managed address where your host offers one.

- **One email thread is one session.** A reply in the thread continues it.
- **When the agent has a question, it asks in its reply.** Your answer in the thread reaches it.
- **Email has no account link.** Choose **Who can email the agent** instead. Kortix drops mail from anyone else.
- **An inbox is private or shared.** A private inbox starts sessions as you. A shared inbox starts project sessions.

### Examples

- **Acme's sales channel.** A rep mentions the bot: "Summarize this week's new leads and flag the top five." The agent replies in the thread with its steps, then the list.
- **Northwind's finance team in Teams.** The agent prepares a vendor payment and posts an approval card. The finance manager selects **Approve** without leaving Teams.
- **A support inbox at Acme.** Customers' emails reach a shared inbox restricted to Acme's own domain. Each thread is a session, and the agent drafts the reply in the thread.
- **A private direct message.** A Northwind manager messages the bot directly to draft a performance note. The session stays private to that manager.

### Tips

- Use **Approval** or **Owner only** in channels where conversations may hold sensitive details.
- Pin an agent to a channel so everyone there gets the same specialist. In Slack, the bare `/kortix` opens the channel panel to change the agent, model and policy.
- Restrict a shared email inbox to your company's domain. An inbox without a list accepts mail from every sender.
- `/kortix whoami` in Slack or `/whoami` in Teams shows which Kortix account you are linked as.
- Telegram is also available as an experimental channel. See the developer reference.

## Models

*Choose which AI models your agents use: Kortix-managed models, your own provider keys, or a ChatGPT subscription, per project, agent or session.*

A model is the AI that an agent thinks with. Kortix does not lock you to one vendor. You can run on Kortix-managed models with no setup, connect your own provider keys, or use a ChatGPT subscription you already pay for. You choose a default for the project, a model per agent, or a different model for one session. Every model request goes through one Kortix gateway, so the same budgets, access rules and cost reports apply whichever model you pick.

### What you can do

- **Start with no setup.** On Kortix Cloud, a new session runs on the platform default model. You connect nothing.
- **Bring your own provider.** Connect a key from a provider such as Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Mistral or Amazon Bedrock.
- **Use your ChatGPT subscription.** Connect a ChatGPT Plus or Pro account and run on your plan.
- **Pick the right model for the work.** Choose a model per session, pin one to an agent, or set a project default.
- **Set how hard the model thinks.** Use the thinking control in the composer when a model offers reasoning levels.
- **Keep a backup.** Set a fallback list, so another model takes over when the first one fails.
- **Cap spend.** Set a budget per project or per member, and get warned or blocked at the limit.
- **Limit what people may use.** Hide models from the picker, or turn off a provider or a model completely.

### How it works

#### Kortix-managed models

Kortix-managed models need no key. The model picker lists them under **Kortix**, with each model's context size and image support.

- Managed models need the Credits plan or Enterprise. They are not available on Free.
- Managed requests use credits from your account's wallet. See [Billing](/docs/#billing).
- Managed models run only through inference providers with zero data retention.
- The managed lineup has no OpenAI or Anthropic models. Use your own key or a ChatGPT subscription for those.
- A self-hosted Kortix has no managed models. Connect your own providers there.

#### Your own keys and subscriptions

When a request runs on your own key or subscription, your provider bills it. Every plan, Free included, can use your own key or a ChatGPT subscription. For any Kortix fee on your own keys, see [Plans & credits](/docs/#billing).

By default, a provider key belongs to the whole project. A project can turn on per-person keys with a feature flag. Then each member can add keys and ChatGPT accounts for their own use, and choose to share them.

A shared session, an automation, or a Slack or Teams conversation uses only keys shared with the whole project. Your private keys stay in your private sessions.

#### Which model a session uses

A session that names no model uses the first of these that is set:

1. The model chosen for the session, the [channel](/docs/#channels) or the [automation](/docs/#automations).
2. The model pinned to the agent.
3. The project default.
4. The account default, for every project that has no default of its own.
5. The platform default.

A default that cannot run, for example after its key was removed, falls back to another connected provider, then to the platform default. A stale default never fails a session.

### Set it up

<Steps>
<Step title="Run on the default">
Start a session. On Kortix Cloud it runs on the platform default model.
</Step>
<Step title="Pick another model">
Choose a model in the composer's model picker. A stopped session uses the new model when it starts again. Switching a running session restarts the agent on its computer and ends the turn in progress.
</Step>
<Step title="Connect your own provider">
Open **Customize → Models → Providers** and paste a key. For a ChatGPT subscription, choose **Use your ChatGPT subscription** in the model picker, then **Connect ChatGPT**.
</Step>
<Step title="Set a budget">
Open **Customize → Models → Costs**. Set a limit per day, week or month. Choose **Block** to refuse new requests at the limit, or **Warn only** to let them run.
</Step>
</Steps>

Account owners, admins and project managers get an email at 80% and at 100% of a budget.

#### Fallback models

Open **Customize → Models → Routing** to list up to 8 models that the gateway tries in order when a model fails. When a fallback model answers, the composer shows **Running on** and its name. A request on your own key moves to a Kortix-billed model only when your own fallback list names that model.

### Examples

- **Acme** runs everyday research and drafting on the Kortix-managed default, with a monthly project budget set to **Block**.
- **Northwind's legal team** pins a model from its own Anthropic key to its contract-review agent, so every review runs on that model.
- **A consultant at Acme** connects a personal ChatGPT subscription for private sessions. Shared sessions keep using the project's keys.
- **Northwind** wants to run only on its own providers. It sets a project default on its own provider first, then disables the **Kortix** provider.

### Tips

- Match the model to the work: send easy tasks to a smaller model.
- Stay in one session for related work. Repeated turns in one session reuse the prompt cache.
- Set a budget before you open a project to a large team.
- To stop a model being used, disable it. Hiding it from the picker only changes the list.

## Teams

*Invite your team, decide who can do what in each project, share sessions, and approve agents' work before it reaches the company repository.*

Kortix is built for a whole company, not one person. An account holds your projects and the people who work in them. You decide who can open each project, which agents they can use, and who sees each session. Agents do not change the company's shared setup on their own. Their work arrives as a change request, and a person approves it.

### How it works

- **Account.** Your company's home in Kortix. It holds projects, members and settings. One login can belong to several accounts.
- **Project.** A workspace with its own agents, skills, connectors and files. Each project is one git repository.
- **Account role.** Each person has one: owner, admin or member.
- **Project role.** Each person has a role in each project they work in: manager or member.
- **Group.** A named set of people. Give a group access, and every person in it gets it.

#### Account roles

| Role | What they can do |
| --- | --- |
| Owner | Everything an admin can do. They can also delete the account and make other people owners. |
| Admin | Invite and remove members, change roles, manage groups, create projects and change the plan. They act as manager on every project. |
| Member | Work in the projects they are given. They see the plan, but they cannot create projects or change billing. |

#### Project roles

| Role | What they can do |
| --- | --- |
| Manager | Everything in the project: edit agents, skills, connectors, secrets and triggers, merge work, and manage access. |
| Member | Open the project, start and follow sessions, and send work for review. They cannot change settings or merge code. |

Members are unlimited and cost nothing extra. See [Billing](/docs/#billing).

### Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a team account first">
    The account Kortix creates when you sign up is personal. It cannot use single sign-on, and a project cannot move to another account later. Open the project menu, select **Switch Project**, then **Create an account…**. Create your team's projects there.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Invite your teammates">
    Open **Account settings** → **Members** and select **Invite**. Type their email addresses and pick a role. A person with a Kortix login joins at once. Everyone else gets an email invite that stays valid for 14 days.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give them a project">
    Open **Account settings** → **Projects**, pick the project, and select **Grant access**. Pick **Manager** or **Member**. You can also set a date when the access ends.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose their agents">
    In the same dialog, set **Agents** to **All agents** or **Only these…**. A member without any agent cannot start a session.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Owners and admins reach every project without a grant.

### Groups

A group saves you from granting access one person at a time. Create an "Engineering" group, give it a project, and everyone in it gets that project. A group only adds access. It never takes away access a person already has.

Creating groups and adding people to them is an Enterprise feature. If your company uses an identity provider, it can keep your groups in sync for you. See [Security](/docs/#security).

### Who sees what

A member sees only themselves in the member list. They do not see other members or groups. Owners and admins see everyone. In a project, people still appear where their work shows, for example as the owner of a session.

A person who opens a project they cannot read sees **Request access**. Kortix tells the project's managers, who approve or reject it.

### Share a session

A session is private to the person who started it. The owner chooses who else can open it:

| Option | Who can open it |
| --- | --- |
| **Only you** | The owner. This is the default. |
| **Specific people** | The owner, plus the members and groups they pick. |
| **Whole project** | Every member of the project. |

Everyone with access can read the conversation and continue it. Only the owner can change who has access.

You can also create a **Public link**. It shows the conversation, read-only, to anyone with the link, without sign-in. You can revoke it at any time.

### How agents' work reaches the company repository

Your company's agents, skills and settings live in one shared repository. Kortix keeps it safe from unreviewed changes:

1. Each session works on its own branch, a private copy of the repository.
2. When the agent finishes, it opens a change request. A change request asks to merge that branch into the shared one.
3. The change request appears on the **Review** page.
4. A person reads the changes and approves, closes, or asks for changes.
5. A note that asks for changes goes back to the agent as a new message.

An agent cannot merge its own change request unless you give it that permission on purpose. A merge that changes agents or triggers also needs the matching permission.

### Examples

- **A new hire.** Acme invites a new support lead as a member. An admin gives her the Support project as a member, with only the support agent. She starts sessions on day one, but she cannot change the agent's setup.
- **A reviewed improvement.** A Northwind agent finds a better way to triage tickets and updates its own skill. It opens a change request. The project manager reads it on **Review** and merges it. From then on, every new session uses the better skill.

### Tips

- Create the team account before you create projects. A project cannot move between accounts.
- Use groups instead of one-by-one grants once your team grows.
- Share a session with **Whole project** when the whole team should follow the work.
- Keep merge rights with people until you trust an agent's changes.
- When someone leaves, remove them from **Members**. Their personal keys and the tokens of their running sessions stop working at once.

## Security

*How Kortix keeps your company's data and agents safe: isolated machines, controlled secrets, held connector credentials, permissions, an audit log, single sign-on and self-hosting.*

Agents do real work with real company data. So Kortix puts limits around every step. Each session runs on its own isolated machine. Secrets and connector credentials are stored encrypted, and you decide who can use them. Every agent has its own permissions. An audit log records who did what. Larger companies can add single sign-on and directory sync, or run Kortix on their own servers.

### How it works

#### Each session has its own machine

Every session starts its own isolated machine, called a sandbox. The agent works there on its own branch of the project's repository. By default, a session can push only to its own branch. Its work reaches the shared repository only when a person merges its change request. See [Work as a team](/docs/#teams).

#### Agents have their own limits

A session runs with the agent's permissions, not the permissions of the person who started it. You decide what each agent may do. An agent can never give itself a permission it does not hold.

#### Secrets: you choose who uses them

A secret is a password, API key or token that a session needs. Kortix encrypts every value. No page, command or API ever shows a stored value again.

Each secret has an audience:

| Choice | Who can use it |
| --- | --- |
| Everyone in the project | Every session in the project. This is the default. |
| **Only you** | You, and your own private sessions. |
| **Specific people, groups, or agents** | Only the people, groups and agents you pick. |

You also choose how a secret reaches the machine:

- **Environment variable.** The machine gets the real value. Code in the session can read it.
- **Enforced.** The machine gets a stand-in that is worth nothing on its own. Kortix swaps in the real value outside the machine, only on requests to the web addresses you list.
- **Disabled.** The value stays on file and reaches no session.

<Callout type="warning">
A secret delivered as an environment variable is a real value inside the session. Use the enforced option for web API keys when code does not need the real value.
</Callout>

#### Connector credentials stay with Kortix

When you connect an app such as Gmail or Linear, Kortix stores the credential. The credential never enters the session's machine. For each call, Kortix picks the account, applies your rules, makes the call and records it.

Your rules decide what each agent may do with each app. An action can **Allow**, **Ask** for a person's approval, or **Block**. Approvals wait on the **Review** page.

Kortix also refuses connector requests to private or internal addresses.

### Audit log

The audit log records who did what in the account, when, how they signed in, and whether it was allowed. Recording is always on. A refused request is recorded too. When an agent acts, the log also names the person it acted for. It never stores a key itself.

Owners and admins read the log under **Account settings** → **Audit log**. Recent events stay readable for 90 days. Events from 90 to 365 days old stay in a locked archive that nobody can shorten or delete, and you can export them. You can also stream every new event to your security tools.

Reading, exporting and streaming the log is an Enterprise feature.

### Sign-in controls

Owners and admins set the account's sign-in rules under **Account settings → Settings → Security**:

- **Require two-factor authentication** for every member.
- **Maximum session length** and **Idle timeout** sign people out after a set time.
- **Active sessions** lists recent sign-ins. You can sign anyone out.

Each person can see and sign out their own devices under **Settings → Security**.

### Single sign-on and directory sync

With single sign-on (SAML), your team signs in through your company's identity provider. Turn on **Enforce SSO for this domain**, and other ways to sign in for that domain stop working.

Directory sync (SCIM) lets your identity provider add, update and remove members and groups in Kortix. When someone leaves your company, they lose access in Kortix too. Set up single sign-on first.

Both are Enterprise features. Set them up under **Account settings** → **Identity**. They need a team account, not the personal account Kortix creates at sign-up.

### Run Kortix on your own infrastructure

You can run the full Kortix platform on a server you control. This is called self-hosting. The web app, the API and the database run on your server. A command-line tool installs, updates and checks it.

Two things to know:

- Sessions still run on cloud sandboxes from Platinum, the reference sandbox provider. They are not on your server.
- Your server must be reachable from the internet, so those sandboxes can call back.

A self-hosted instance has billing off by default. A Kortix Enterprise license turns on the Enterprise features for every account on it.

### Set it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn on two-factor">
    Require two-factor authentication for the whole account under **Account settings → Settings → Security**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Limit each secret">
    On the project's **Secrets** page, give each secret the smallest audience that works.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set connector rules">
    Block actions agents should never take. Ask for approval on risky ones.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect your identity provider">
    With Enterprise, set up single sign-on and directory sync under **Account settings** → **Identity**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Examples

- **Finance data.** Acme stores its billing API token as a secret shared only with its Finance group and its reporting agent. Nobody else's sessions get the value.
- **Email with a human check.** Northwind lets its support agent read the team inbox freely. Every email it sends waits for a person's approval on **Review**.

### Tips

- Prefer connectors over secrets for apps: the credential stays with Kortix.
- Give agents only the permissions their job needs.
- Check the audit log for refused requests after you change access.

## Billing

*The Free and Credits plans, Enterprise, what uses credits, the account wallet, top-ups and plan changes.*

Kortix bills an account through credits. Credits sit in one wallet for the whole account, and every project spends from it. Members are free and unlimited, so adding people never changes the bill. This page explains the plans, what spends credits, and how to add more.

To see your balance, open the project menu → **Switch Project** → **Account settings** → **Billing** → **Plan**.

### Plans

An account is on one of two plans: Free or Credits. See [kortix.com/pricing](https://kortix.com/pricing) for what each plan costs and grants.

| | Free | Credits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Billed | No charge | A monthly amount you choose |
| Credits | A grant every month | 100 credits per dollar of your monthly amount, every billing cycle |
| Members | Unlimited | Unlimited, no per-member charge |
| Kortix-managed models | None | All |
| Buy credits and auto top-up | No | Yes |
| Projects | 1 | 200 |

- Every plan can use models on your own provider key or a ChatGPT subscription. See [Models](/docs/#models).
- On Free, credits pay for compute and your own models only.
- Credits tiers run from 5,000 to 1,000,000 credits a month. Every tier has the same rate. A bigger tier buys more credits, never a cheaper credit.

#### Enterprise

Enterprise is not a plan. It turns on extra features on either plan: single sign-on, directory sync, groups and custom roles, the audit log, and branding. With an Enterprise license, an account has no project limit. To get one, select **Talk to sales** in the app, or email [support@kortix.com](mailto:support@kortix.com). See [Security](/docs/#security).

### What uses credits

| What | How it is counted |
| --- | --- |
| Kortix-managed models | The tokens each request uses, at the provider's base price plus a 5.5% platform fee. |
| Models on your own key | Nothing for the first $1,000 of usage a month. After that, 5% of each request's list price. A ChatGPT subscription is never charged. |
| Session machines | The size the machine reserves, for the seconds it runs. App machines count too. |
| Web tools | Each web search, image search or web scraping request that goes through Kortix. |

A stopped or sleeping machine costs nothing. The default machine costs about $0.11 an hour, about 11 credits.

### The wallet

The wallet holds two kinds of credits:

| Kind | Comes from | Expires |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expiring | Your plan's grant | Unused credits roll over for one month. The next grant then replaces them. |
| Non-expiring | Top-ups, bonuses, refunds and promotions | Never |

Kortix spends expiring credits first. Work that already finished is always recorded, even if the balance goes below zero. A new run needs a positive balance.

### Add credits

Only paid plans can buy credits. Purchased credits never expire. A top-up of $50 or more adds a bonus, from +1% up to +5% for $1,000 or more.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add credits once">
    On **Billing → Plan**, select **Add credits**, choose an amount, and pay.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Or turn on auto top-up">
    In **Auto top-up**, set the amount to add and the balance that triggers it. Both are at least 100 credits. Auto top-up needs a saved payment method.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A card payment adds credits at once. A bank debit can take several business days. If an auto top-up charge fails for good, Kortix turns auto top-up off and emails every owner the reason.

### Change your plan

| Change | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Move from Free to Credits | Choose a tier on **Billing → Plan** and select **Subscribe**. |
| Raise the monthly amount | You pay the difference now, for the rest of the cycle, and get that share of the new credits. |
| Lower the monthly amount | It applies from the next renewal. No refund. Credits you have stay. |
| Cancel | **Cancel subscription** ends the plan at the end of the billing period. **Reactivate subscription** undoes it until then. |

Manage the payment method and invoices under **Manage billing**. Only the owner role can change billing by default. Every member can see it. See [Work as a team](/docs/#teams).

### Running out

When the balance reaches zero, agents pause and automations stop starting sessions. The account's Apps stop too, and visitors see an **App paused** page.

To continue, add credits or update the payment method. On Free, subscribe to Credits or wait for the next monthly grant. A member who cannot add credits can select **Ask an owner**. Every owner then gets an email with the balance.

### Track usage

| Where | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Billing → Plan** | Plan, wallet, spend this period, auto top-up |
| **Billing → Usage** | Kortix charges and the credit history |
| **Customize → Models → Costs** in a project | Model spend and budgets for that project |

### Tips

- Send easy tasks to a smaller model.
- Write clear instructions. A well-defined task needs fewer turns.
- Stay in one session for follow-ups. Repeated turns reuse the prompt cache.
- Do not reserve a bigger machine than the work needs.
- Check **Billing → Usage** and fix the costliest sessions first.
- Turn on auto top-up so agents never pause by surprise.

For a billing question, email [support@kortix.com](mailto:support@kortix.com).
