title: Notifications
description: How Kortix tells you that a session or a trigger needs you, and where.

A notification tells you that a session or a trigger needs you. Kortix writes
each one to your inbox, the bell. It then sends it to your phone, your browser,
or your email, as your settings allow.

## Kinds

A turn is one round of agent work, from a message to the agent's last reply.

| Kind | Sent when | Push by default | Email by default |
|---|---|---|---|
| `turn_done` | A turn ends. | On | Off |
| `turn_error` | A turn ends with an error. | On | Digest |
| `question` | The agent asks a question and waits for the answer. | On | Digest |
| `permission` | The agent asks for permission to run a tool. | On | Never |
| `shared` | Someone shares a session with you. | On | Digest |
| `automation_failed` | A trigger or a reminder starts failing. | On | At once |
| `automation_recovered` | That trigger or reminder works again. | On | Off |

Turn on **Email** for `turn_done` and it joins the digest. Turn it on for
`automation_recovered` and Kortix emails it at once. A permission request is
never emailed: it goes stale before a digest is due.

## Who gets a notification

### Session events

Two groups of people get a session's notifications.

- **The prompter** — the person whose message started the turn.
- **The watchers** — the session's creator, and each person who sent a message
  in it as themselves: from the web app, the desktop app, the mobile app, the
  CLI, or a personal access key. A message an agent sends with its session
  token makes nobody a watcher.

Who gets what depends on how the session started.

| Session | Turn ends | Questions and permission requests |
|---|---|---|
| Started in the web app, the desktop app, the mobile app, the CLI, or the API | The prompter and the watchers | The prompter and the watchers |
| Started from Slack, Microsoft Teams, or email | The prompter, in the bell only | See [Slack, Teams, and email sessions](#slack-teams-and-email-sessions) |
| Started by a trigger | The prompter only. A fire has no prompter: its failed run raises an [alert](/docs/connect/triggers#alerts) instead. | The prompter and the trigger's alert recipients |
| A worker session another session started | Nobody. The parent session reports. | The prompter and the watchers |

These rules apply to every row:

- A person who [muted the session](#mute-a-session) gets nothing from it.
- A person who cannot open the session gets nothing from it.
- A turn you stop yourself sends nothing. When a turn ends and your next queued
  message starts at once, Kortix sends no `turn_done`: the session is still
  working.

### Shared sessions

When the owner shares a session with specific people, each person added gets one
`shared` notification. A person added through a group gets it too. The owner
gets none. Sharing with the whole project sends none. Kortix sends at most one
per person and session per day (UTC), so changing the list again does not
repeat it. See [Session access](/docs/work/sessions#session-access).

### Triggers and reminders

An alert is the `automation_failed` or `automation_recovered` notification of a
trigger or a reminder. It goes to the people who wrote the trigger or set the
reminder. See [Triggers → Alerts](/docs/connect/triggers#alerts) and
[Reminders → Failure alerts](/docs/connect/reminders#failure-alerts).

## Where notifications appear

### The bell

On the web and in the desktop app, the bell sits at the top of the project
sidebar and on the projects page. Its badge counts your unread notifications.
Choose a notification to open its session, or the **Triggers** page for an
alert. That marks it read. **Mark all as read** clears the count.

Opening a session marks its notifications read, on the web, in the desktop app,
and in the mobile app.

### The mobile app

The mobile app gets a push for each notification your **Push** switches allow.
Tap it to open the session, or the project for an alert. **Notifications** in the drawer lists every
notification across your projects.

### Browser notifications

A browser can show notifications with every Kortix tab closed. This uses Web
Push.

1. Open **Settings → Notifications**.
2. Turn on **Enable notifications**.
3. Allow notifications when the browser asks.

Click a notification to open its session. The setting is per browser: turn it on
in each browser you use. Signing out of Kortix in that browser stops its
notifications.

Web Push works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari on macOS. Another browser
shows notifications only while a Kortix tab is open.

### The desktop app

The desktop app has no push service. While its window is open, it checks for
new notifications about once a minute. When the window is not in focus, it shows
each new one as an OS notification, with **Enable notifications** on. With the
window closed, it shows nothing.

### Email

- An `automation_failed` alert is emailed at once.
- `turn_error`, `question`, and `shared` go into a digest. A notification that is
  still unread 15 minutes after it arrives joins your next digest.
- Kortix sends you at most one digest per hour. A digest lists up to 10
  notifications and counts the rest.
- A digest leaves out a question that someone already answered, and anything you
  can no longer open.

Email needs an email provider on the deployment. Kortix Cloud has one. A
[self-hosted](/docs/host) deployment sends email once its operator sets
`EMAIL_URL`. Without a provider, the **Email** switches do not show.

## When a session is open

- A notification that arrives while you have its session open is marked read at
  once. It never joins a digest.
- A session open in a tab with **Enable notifications** on shows its own OS
  notification. Kortix then sends no mobile push and no Web Push for that
  session. A tab without browser notifications does not hold back the push.

## Choose what you get

Open **Settings → Notifications**. Each kind has a **Push** switch and an
**Email** switch.

- The switches follow you: they apply in every account, on every phone, and in
  every browser.
- **Push** covers the mobile app, Web Push, and the notifications an open tab
  shows.
- The bell lists every notification, whatever the switches say.
- The mobile app's **Settings → Notifications** shows the **Push** switches.
  Change **Email** on the web. The mobile **Notifications** and **Play sound**
  switches apply to that phone only.

## Mute a session

Open the session menu next to the session's title and choose **Mute
notifications**. You then get nothing from that session: no turn ends, no
questions, and no permission requests, even for turns you prompt. A new message
from you does not unmute it.

Choose **Unmute notifications** in the same menu to get them again. On a session
you do not watch, such as one shared with you, it makes you a watcher.

## Slack, Teams, and email sessions

A session that started from a Slack thread, a Teams chat, or an email replies
there. That reply is the notification.

- A turn end sends no push and no email. Only the prompter gets it, as a row in
  the bell. The creator and the watchers get nothing.
- A question from a Slack or Teams session posts in the thread. If Kortix cannot
  post it there, the prompter and the watchers get it as a notification.
- A permission request from a Slack or Teams session also reaches the prompter
  and the watchers.
- An email session sends every question and permission request to the prompter
  and the watchers. The email channel is experimental. See
  [Slack & channels](/docs/connect/slack#live-channels).

## Privacy and retention

- Kortix writes a notification only for a person who can open its session, or
  who can read the project's triggers for an alert.
- A notification follows your access. When you lose access to its session or
  project, it leaves your bell and your digests.
- Leaving an account, or removal from it, deletes your notifications in that
  account. You stop watching its sessions and triggers.
- In an account that requires two-factor authentication, the bell shows that
  account's notifications only after you sign in with your second factor. A
  browser signed in without it gets no Web Push for that account.
- A push and an email carry the session title and a short detail, such as the
  agent's question or the error.
- Signing out a device under **Settings → Security → Devices** stops its Web
  Push. Deleting your Kortix account deletes your notifications and your
  settings.
- Kortix deletes notifications after 90 days.

## API

These routes act for a person: a browser sign-in or a personal access key. An
agent's session token and a service account get `403`.

| Route | Effect |
|---|---|
| `GET /v1/notifications` | Lists your newest notifications. `limit` takes 1–50 (default 20). `before=<id>` reads the next page. Returns `notifications`, `unread_count` (counts up to 100), and `next_before`. |
| `POST /v1/notifications/read` | Marks notifications read. Body: exactly one of `{ "ids": [...] }` (1–100 ids), `{ "all": true }`, or `{ "session_id": "..." }`. Returns `updated` and `unread_count`. |
| `GET /v1/notifications/preferences` | Returns `kinds` (the **Push** and **Email** switch of each kind) and `email_available`. |
| `PUT /v1/notifications/preferences` | Changes the switches you send, for example `{ "kinds": { "turn_done": { "push": false } } }`. Returns the same shape as `GET`. |
| `GET /v1/projects/:projectId/sessions/:sessionId/watch` | Returns `{ "watching": true }` when you watch the session. |
| `PUT /v1/projects/:projectId/sessions/:sessionId/watch` | Body `{ "watching": false }` mutes the session. `{ "watching": true }` watches it. |

`GET /v1/notifications/web-push/key` and
`POST`/`DELETE /v1/notifications/web-push/subscriptions` register a browser for
Web Push. The web app calls them. They accept a browser sign-in only. The SDK
wraps every route above: see [SDK reference](/docs/sdk/reference).
