An account holds projects and the people who work in them. Every person holds one account role, and a project role on each project they work in. This page is for the owners and admins who invite a team and decide who can do what.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Invite a teammate">
    Open **Account settings** → **Members** and select **Invite**. From a terminal:

    ```bash
    kortix members invite alice@example.com --role member --project <project_id>:member
    ```

    `--project` gives the project role in the same call. Step 3 still applies.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give them a project">
    Open **Account settings** → **Projects**, pick the project, and select **Grant access**. Pick a role: **Manager** or **Member**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose their agents">
    Give them at least one agent. In the **Grant access** dialog, set **Agents** to **All agents** or **Only these…**. From a terminal:

    ```bash
    kortix access grant --user alice@example.com --agent kortix   # one person
    kortix access grant --everyone --agent kortix                 # every project member
    ```

    Without an agent grant, a `member` cannot start a session: the API answers `403` with "You don't have access to any agent in this project."
  </Step>
</Steps>

Owners and admins reach every project without a grant.

## Accounts

Kortix creates a personal account when you sign up. You are its owner and its [super-admin](#super-admin). Invite people to work in it with you.

- **Several accounts, one login.** One login can belong to many accounts. Each account keeps its own projects, members and settings.
- **Switch.** Open the project menu → **Switch Project**. It lists your projects grouped by account. In the CLI, `kortix accounts use <slug|id>` sets the account that account-scoped commands act on.
- **Create a team account first.** The account Kortix creates at sign-up is personal. It cannot hold SAML SSO (`403` `sso_personal_account`), and a project cannot move to another account. For a team, select **Create an account…** before you create projects.
- **Create another.** Open the project menu → **Switch Project** → **Create an account…**. The creator becomes owner and super-admin. A self-hosted instance can restrict this to its server admin; the API then returns `403` `account_creation_restricted`. See [Self-hosting](/docs/host).
- **Rename.** **Account settings** → **Settings**. Needs `account.write`.

```bash
kortix accounts ls          # the accounts you belong to
kortix accounts use         # pick the active account (interactive)
kortix accounts current     # print the active account
kortix accounts info        # one account in detail
```

## Account roles

Each person holds exactly one account role: `owner`, `admin` or `member`.

| Role | Can do |
| --- | --- |
| `owner` | Everything an admin can do, plus: delete the account (`account.delete`), grant or revoke super-admin (`member.super_admin.grant`), and grant or change the `owner` role. |
| `admin` | Rename the account and change its security settings, except **Admins can open every session**. Change the plan and payment method (`billing.write`). Invite, remove and change the role of members. Manage groups, roles, assignments, service-account keys and OAuth clients. Create projects. Read the audit log and the plan. |
| `member` | See the account, its plan and its billing ledger (`billing.read`). In the member list they see only themselves, and they see no groups (see [Who sees the member list](#who-sees-the-member-list)). Create, list and revoke their own personal access keys. Work only in the projects a grant gives them. |

- **Members cannot create projects** and cannot change billing. Owners and admins hold `billing.write`. Only an owner holds `account.delete`.
- **Owners and admins are manager on every project** in the account. This is a rule of the account role, not a grant, and a project grant cannot lower it. To limit an owner or admin on one project, change their account role to `member` first.
- **Promotion removes project grants.** When a person becomes owner or admin, Kortix deletes their direct project grants because the account role already covers every project.
- **Only an owner touches owners.** An admin who tries to remove an owner gets `403`. Only an owner can grant the `owner` role or change an owner's role.
- **The last owner stays.** Removing, demoting or leaving as the last owner returns `409`. Make another member owner first.
- **An account role belongs to a person.** Binding `owner`, `admin` or `member` to a group returns `400`. Give a group a project role or a custom role instead.

Opening another person's private session is a separate, owner-controlled setting. See [Who can open a session](/docs/work/sessions#who-can-open-a-session).

### Who sees the member list

Owners and admins see every member and every group. A `member` sees nobody else:

- **Members** lists only themselves, not even the owners. `kortix members` does the same.
- They see no group, not even their own. **Groups** is hidden, `kortix groups` lists none, and reading any group answers `404`.
- A project's access list shows only themselves. A `member` who manages that project also sees the people and groups with access to it, so they can manage them, and still nobody without access.
- The Files share dialog names only themselves.
- **Session costs** list only their own sessions. Asking for another person's costs answers `403`, and another person's session answers `404`.
- In a project they do not manage, branches, change requests, the review inbox, gateway logs and spend show only their own work and the sessions they may open. Another member's private session leaves no trace there.
- SSO group mappings list none.
- **Usage by project** lists only the projects they can read.
- **API keys** (`GET /v1/accounts/tokens`) list only their own keys and their sessions' tokens. Only a caller with `token.revoke` lists every key in the account.
- Service accounts and OAuth clients answer `403`: reading them needs `token.create`, the same leaf that creates them.

Another member's access details (**View access**, `GET .../iam/members/:userId/...`) and the MFA enforcement preview stay with owners and admins. People you work with in a shared session or project still appear where their work does: as the author of a message, the owner of a session, or a person a secret is shared with.

To show the whole directory to more people, create a custom account role with `member.read` (every member) and `group.read` (every group), and assign it to those people or to a group. See [Custom roles](#custom-roles).

## Members and invites

### Invite people

1. Open **Account settings** → **Members** and select **Invite**.
2. Type one or more email addresses.
3. Pick the account role: **Admin** or **Member**.
4. For a member, optionally add projects and a project role for each.

What happens next depends on the address:

- **The address has a Kortix login.** Kortix adds the person at once, with the project roles you picked.
- **The address has no login.** Kortix emails an invite link. The invite is valid for 14 days. The project roles apply when the person accepts.

A self-hosted instance with no email provider confirms sign-ups without an email, so a password login does not prove its address there. Kortix adds such a login at once only when it is already a member of the account. Otherwise it creates an invite, and the login with that address accepts it from the project chooser or the **Switch Project** menu. That login is whoever signed up first with the address. See [Unconfirmed email addresses](/docs/host#unconfirmed-email-addresses).

Project roles in an invite apply to `member` invites only. Kortix ignores them on an `admin` invite, because an admin already reaches every project. To make someone owner, invite them as admin or member, then change their role.

### Accept an invite

The invitee opens the link in the email and signs in or signs up with the invited address. Then they select accept or decline. Accept opens the first project the invite grants, or the account they joined.

| Response | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `403` | The signed-in address is not the invited address. |
| `410` | The invite expired, or it was already used. Ask for a new one. |
| `409` | Another login already accepted this invite. |
| `403` `sso_email_domain_unverified` | The invitee signed in through an SSO provider that has not verified their email's domain. Sign in with the email address instead. |

In the mobile app, the project switcher lists pending invites under **Invites**. With no project to reopen, the app opens a pending invite first.

### Manage invites and members

- **Pending invites** show on **Members**. Resending one sends the email again and starts a new 14-day window. Cancelling one revokes the link.
- **Edit access** in a member's row menu changes their account role. To change several members at once, select their rows and select **Change role**.
- **Remove from account** removes the person's role, project grants and group memberships. It revokes their personal access keys and the tokens of their running sessions at once. See [When a person leaves](#when-a-person-leaves).

### When a person leaves

Removal, [Leave](#leave) and SCIM deactivation (`active: false` or `DELETE`, see [SCIM provisioning](/docs/accounts/identity#scim-provisioning)) end the same things in the account, at once:

- the person's roles, project grants and group memberships;
- their personal access keys and the tokens of their running sessions;
- their notifications and follows in the account's projects;
- every computer account in the account that reaches a machine they own. This covers a computer they shared with a project and a computer they paired for themselves. The account becomes `revoked`, with `metadata.revokedReason: owner_left`. The computer stays theirs and keeps working in their other Kortix accounts;
- every reminder they set in the account's projects. The reminder becomes `paused`. A reminder that a session's agent set keeps firing;
- their seat on a per-seat plan. Their personal drive folders pass to an owner.

The audit log records one `member.offboarded` event, titled **Offboarded a member**, per departure. Its `metadata` holds `reason` (`removed`, `left` or `deprovisioned`), `computers_revoked` and `reminders_disabled`. A count is `0` when the person had nothing of that kind, and `null` when that step failed.

### Errors

| Code | Status | Cause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `account_identity_ambiguous` | `409` | More than one identity in the account uses the email address. |
| — | `409` | The person is already a member. |

Members are unlimited and cost nothing extra. The account shares one credit wallet. See [Plan changes](/docs/credits#plan-changes).

### Members from the CLI

```bash
kortix members ls
kortix members invite alice@example.com --role member --project <project_id>:manager
kortix members set-role alice@example.com --role admin
kortix members rm alice@example.com
kortix members invites ls
kortix members invites resend <invite_id>
kortix members invites cancel <invite_id>
```

`--project <project_id>:<role>` repeats, one per project. `invite` accepts `admin` or `member`. `set-role` accepts `owner`, `admin` or `member`.

## Project access

A project has two roles.

| Role | Can do |
| --- | --- |
| `manager` | Every project permission: edit the project and its agents, skills, connectors, secrets, models, triggers and Apps; push and merge; act on reviews; manage access; issue project credentials; delete the project. |
| `member` | Read and run: open the project, see its agents, commands, Apps, members, triggers, reviews and git history; start, follow and stop sessions; fire triggers; submit work for review; read gateway logs and spend. |

A `member` cannot:

- edit the project, its settings or its sandbox, or delete it;
- read or change secrets, connectors, skills, models or workspace files;
- push or merge code, act on a change request, or delete a git ref;
- create, edit or delete triggers;
- manage access, gateway keys or budgets, or read usage;
- create, edit or deploy Apps;
- bind a session to a channel, or issue a project credential.

The API rejects `editor` with `400`. It reads `viewer` and `user` as `member`. The exact permissions of each role are in [Permissions](#permissions).

### Grant project access

1. Open **Account settings** → **Projects** and pick the project.
2. Select **Grant access**.
3. In **Grant to**, pick people or groups, or type an email to invite someone new.
4. Pick the **Role**.
5. Set **Agents**: **All agents** or **Only these…**.
6. Optionally set **Expires**. The grant ends at that time.

Inviting an email address outside the account creates an account invite with the `member` role and this project role. A person who already has a login joins the account as `member` at once.

### Access requests

A signed-in person who opens a project they cannot read sees **Request access**, with an optional note to the managers. Kortix notifies the project's managers. One person can send 10 requests per hour, and one project receives at most 30 per hour.

A manager approves the request with a role (`member` by default) or rejects it. Approving grants the role at once.

### Project access from the CLI

```bash
kortix access ls                                 # members and their project roles
kortix access invite bob@example.com --role member
kortix access grant <user_id> --role manager     # set a member's project role
kortix access revoke <user_id>                   # remove their project access
kortix access pending                            # pending project invites
kortix access resend <invite_id>                 # resend, new 14-day window
kortix access cancel <invite_id>
kortix access requests ls
kortix access requests approve <request_id> --role member
kortix access requests reject <request_id>
```

These commands act on the linked project, or on `--project <project_id>`. `ls` needs `project.members.read`. Every other command needs `project.members.manage`. `invite` and `grant` take `--expires <iso>`.

## Groups

A group is a named set of people. Grant a role to a group, and every member of the group holds it. A group adds access. It never removes access a person already holds.

- **Entitlement.** Creating a group, renaming it and adding members need the Enterprise `rbac` entitlement. Without it the API returns `402` `entitlement_required`. Reading groups, removing members and deleting a group stay open, so an account can clean up after a plan change. Any other edit to a group, including its description, needs the entitlement.
- **Delete.** Deleting a group removes every grant it carried.
- **Names.** A group name is unique in the account. A duplicate returns `409`.
- **IdP-owned groups.** A group that SCIM provisioned belongs to the identity provider. Renaming it or changing its members returns `409` `group_idp_managed`: change it in the IdP. Description edits and deletion stay allowed. See [SCIM provisioning](/docs/accounts/identity#scim-provisioning).

Give a group a project in **Account settings** → **Projects** → **Grant access**, or from the CLI:

```bash
kortix groups create Engineering --description "Everyone who ships code"
kortix groups add Engineering alice@example.com bob@example.com
kortix access grant --group <group_id> --role member --project <project_id>
kortix groups projects Engineering        # which projects it reaches, at which role
kortix groups members Engineering
kortix groups remove Engineering bob@example.com
kortix groups rm Engineering
```

## Grant access to one object

Kortix has one grant record: an **assignment**. Every grant on this page is an assignment.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Principal | Who holds it: `user`, `group`, `service_account` (an agent's identity or a machine key), `pending` (an invited email), or `project` (everyone in the project). |
| Role | A built-in or custom role. |
| Scope | `account` (every project) or `project` (one project). |
| Object | Optional. One `agent`, `connection` (a shared connector account), `secret` value or Files `folder` inside the project. |
| `expires_at` | Optional. The assignment stops granting at that time. The row stays for the audit trail. |

An object assignment carries no permission of its own. It narrows *which objects* a person reaches with the permissions their role gives. An agent, connector-account or secret grant uses the marker role `agent-user`. A Files folder grant uses `folder-reader`, `folder-writer` or `folder-manager`.

### Agents

Agents are closed by default. A `member` reaches an agent only when a grant names them, one of their groups, or everyone in the project. A `manager` reaches every agent until the first grant names that agent. After the first grant, only the named principals reach it, managers included. Account owners and admins reach every agent.

Giving a person an agent also lets them use the secrets and connectors the agent declares. They use them through the agent; they cannot edit them.

### Connector accounts and secret values

A shared connector account and a secret value are open to everyone in the project until their first grant. After the first grant, only the named people, groups and agents can use it. Removing the last grant opens it to the project again. Only a person can change these grants; an agent session gets `403`.

- Connector accounts: [Share accounts and give agents access](/docs/connect/connectors#share-accounts-and-give-agents-access).
- Secrets: [Who can use it](/docs/project/secrets#who-can-use-it).

### Everyone in the project

Pick **Everyone in** followed by the project name to give an agent or a connector account to every project member at once. This principal (`project`) holds object grants only, never a role. Each person keeps their own project role.

### Object grants from the CLI

`kortix access grant` is the one command for object grants:

```bash
kortix access grant --user alice@example.com --agent support-bot
kortix access grant --group <group_id> --agent support-bot --expires 2026-12-31T00:00:00Z
kortix access grant --everyone --agent support-bot
kortix access grant --group <group_id> --connection <connection_id>
kortix access assignments                 # assignments on the linked project
kortix access assignments --all           # every assignment in the account
kortix access revoke <assignment_id>
```

`kortix grants assign <agent> --to <email>` writes the same agent grant.

The API route is `POST /v1/accounts/:accountId/iam/assignments`. The permission it needs follows what you grant:

| Grant | Needs |
| --- | --- |
| Project role, or an agent grant | `project.members.manage` on that project |
| Connector account grant | `project.connector.connections.manage` |
| Secret grant | `project.secret.write` |
| Account role | `member.update`, plus owner rights for `owner` |
| Custom role | `policy.create` and the `rbac` entitlement |

### Check why someone has access

- **In the web app:** open the member's menu on **Members** and select **View access**. Without `member.read`, it opens only on your own row.
- **Over the API:** `GET /v1/accounts/:accountId/iam/members/:userId/effective?action=project.secret.write&resourceType=project&resourceId=<project_id>` returns `allowed` and a `reason`, such as `project_role_insufficient` or `resource_scope_insufficient`. `POST .../effective:batch` checks up to 64 actions in one call. Asking about yourself answers with your real credential; asking about someone else answers from their roles.

### Per-feature access settings

Some features carry their own setting for one resource. Each narrows access. None grants a permission the role denies.

| Setting | Decides |
| --- | --- |
| [App access](/docs/apps#access) | Who can open one deployed App |
| [Trigger session access](/docs/connect/triggers#session-access) | Who can open the sessions one trigger creates |
| [Channel rules](/docs/connect/channels#who-can-do-what) | Who can start a session from a Slack channel |
| [Computer access](/docs/connect/computers#control-access-on-the-machine) | Which calls a connected computer accepts |

### Agents have their own ceiling

A session runs with the agent's permissions, not the permissions of the person who started it. Two limits apply: the role bound to the agent's service account, and the agent's `kortix_permissions` in `kortix.yaml`. Neither widens the other. See [What an agent may do](/docs/project/agents#what-an-agent-may-do).

## Custom roles

A custom role is a role your account defines from catalog permissions. It needs the Enterprise `rbac` entitlement: creating, editing or assigning one without it returns `402` `entitlement_required`.

- **Only adds.** A custom role grants permissions. Kortix has no deny rule.
- **One scope.** A role is account-scoped or project-scoped. It holds only permissions of its scope.
- **No escalation.** A custom role cannot hold a permission marked *no* under "Custom role" in [Permissions](#permissions). Owner and admin powers stay with the built-in roles.
- **Key and name.** The key is 2 to 64 characters of `a-z`, `0-9` and `_`. It never changes. The name is up to 128 characters.
- **Delete.** Deleting a custom role revokes every assignment of it.
- **Built-in roles** are fixed. Editing or deleting one returns `400`.

Create and edit roles in **Account settings** → **Roles**, or from the CLI:

```bash
kortix roles create support_lead --name "Support lead" --scope project \
  --actions project.read,project.session.start,project.trigger.fire,project.members.manage
kortix roles set-actions support_lead --actions project.read,project.session.start
kortix roles edit support_lead --name "Support leads"
kortix roles show support_lead              # permissions and usage
kortix access grant --group <group_id> --role support_lead --project <project_id>
kortix roles rm support_lead
```

### Built-in roles

| Key | Scope | Grants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `owner` | account | Full account control |
| `admin` | account | Members, groups, roles and keys |
| `member` | account | Baseline membership |
| `manager` | project | Full project control, including members and delete |
| `member` | project | Read, run sessions, fire triggers |
| `agent-user` | project | Nothing. The marker role of an object grant. |
| `folder-reader`, `folder-writer`, `folder-manager` | project | Read, write, or manage one shared Files folder |

`kortix roles ls` lists them with your custom roles. The folder roles are not in that list: a folder's share dialog in Files sets them.

### IAM as code

`kortix roles export` writes your custom roles and their bindings to TOML, or to JSON with `--format json`. `kortix roles import <file>` creates the roles that are missing, then imports the bindings. Bindings name roles by key, so a file moves between accounts.

```bash
kortix roles export --out roles.toml
kortix roles import roles.toml
```

`kortix roles assign`, `unassign` and `assignments`, and the `/iam/policies` routes, are a legacy API over the same assignments. They cover custom roles only. Use `kortix access grant` and `/iam/assignments`.

## Super-admin

Super-admin is a flag on one membership, not a role. A super-admin passes every permission check in the account. Each request a super-admin makes is in the [audit log](#audit-log) like any other request.

- The person who creates an account starts as owner and super-admin.
- Only an owner grants or revokes it: `kortix members super-admin <user> on|off`, or `PATCH /v1/accounts/:accountId/iam/members/:userId/super-admin`.
- Demoting an owner clears their super-admin flag and records `iam.member.super_admin.revoke`.
- A super-admin is exempt from the account's MFA requirement. SSO enforcement still applies. See [Sign-in, SSO & API keys](/docs/accounts/identity).

## Branding

Every account can replace the Kortix marks its members see in the web app. Branding is on for every plan. Open **Account settings** → **Branding**.

| Item | Where it shows |
| --- | --- |
| `logo` | The wide brandmark in the app |
| `icon` | The square mark |
| `favicon` | The browser-tab icon |
| `logo_dark`, `icon_dark`, `favicon_dark` | The same, in dark mode |
| `app_name` | Replaces "Kortix" in the browser tab title. Up to 60 characters. |

- **Formats.** PNG, JPEG, WebP, SVG or ICO, up to 1 MiB each. Kortix rejects an SVG that carries script or other active content.
- **Fallbacks.** A missing logo uses the icon. A missing favicon uses the icon. A missing dark variant uses the dark icon, then the light image.
- **Scope.** Inside a project, members see the brand of the account that owns the project. The favicon follows the operating system's color scheme; the other marks follow the app theme.
- **Kortix stays.** Sign-in pages, emails and public share pages stay Kortix-branded.
- **Plan change.** When the entitlement lapses, members see Kortix again. Nothing is deleted. Removing images and resetting stay available without the entitlement.

Writes need `account.write` and the entitlement (`402` `entitlement_required` otherwise).

## Audit log

The audit log records who did what in the account, when, through which credential, and whether it was allowed. Recording is always on. Reading, exporting and streaming the log need the Enterprise `auditAccess` entitlement.

### What is recorded

Kortix writes one row for every request that reaches the API: HTTP requests, WebSocket upgrades, preview requests, git clones and pushes, and SCIM calls. A refused request is a row with `outcome: denied`. Events outside a request, such as a sandbox reading a secret or a background job expiring a grant, are rows too.

| Field | Values |
| --- | --- |
| `action` | `domain.resource.verb`, for example `iam.group.create`. One per route. |
| `actor_type` | `human`, `agent`, `service_account`, `system`, `anonymous` |
| `credential_kind` | `browser_session`, `personal_access_token`, `oauth_app`, `session_token`, `api_key`, `service_account`, `scim_token`. The web app shows it as **Via**. |
| `credential_id` | The id of the key or session. Never the secret. |
| `outcome` | `success`, `failure`, `denied`, `pending` |
| `metadata.http` | The route template, never the raw path. |

An agent's row also names the person the agent acted for (`on_behalf_of_user_id`). Kortix derives the caller and the credential from what it authenticated, never from a request header. An anonymous request that names one of your projects is written with `actor_type: anonymous`.

Kortix does not record health probes, CORS preflights, or anonymous visitors to a deployed App.

### Read the log

Open **Account settings** → **Audit log**. Reading needs `audit.read` (owners and admins) and the entitlement.

The **Session** filter searches the selected project's sessions that you can open, by name or session-id prefix, on the server. It lists 20 matches at a time: **Load more** reads the next 20. To filter by any other session, paste its full session id into the search field and pick it. A pasted id works with no project selected, for a session you cannot open, and for a deleted session.

```bash
kortix audit ls --since 24h
kortix audit ls --outcome denied --since 7d
kortix audit ls --action iam. --json
kortix audit ls --credential-kind oauth_app --all
kortix audit project <project_id>
kortix audit session <session_id> --project <project_id>
```

| Flag | Filters on |
| --- | --- |
| `--since`, `--until` | Time. ISO-8601, or a span such as `30m`, `24h`, `7d`, `2w`. |
| `--action <prefix>` | An action prefix, such as `iam.` or `session.` |
| `--actor <user_id>`, `--actor-type <t>` | Who acted |
| `--credential-kind <k>` | What the API authenticated |
| `--outcome <o>` | `success`, `failure`, `denied`, `pending` |
| `--project`, `--session` | One project or session |
| `--source`, `--phase`, `--resource-type` | Execution source, lifecycle phase, resource type |
| `--request-id`, `--correlation-id` | One request, or one chain of events |
| `-q <text>` | Free text over action, resource, project and session |
| `--limit`, `--cursor`, `--all` | Paging: 50 per page by default, 200 at most; `--all` follows every cursor |

The API route is `GET /v1/accounts/:accountId/audit` with the same filters as query parameters (`actor_type`, `credential_kind`, and so on).

- **Project log:** `GET /v1/projects/:projectId/audit` needs `project.members.manage` and the entitlement.
- **Session timeline:** `GET /v1/projects/:projectId/sessions/:sessionId/audit` needs `project.session.read`. Without the entitlement it returns only the unresolved pending approvals.

### Who was active, and how often

The audit log names an identity, not a person. A sandbox, the CLI, or a script that holds a person's token writes rows as that person, and those rows can outnumber the rows of what the person did in a browser by 100 to 1 or more. To ask "who used this project, and on which days", read the activity rollup instead of counting log rows.

```bash
kortix audit activity --project <project_id> --since 6w
kortix audit activity --project <project_id> --since 6w --client browser
kortix audit activity --actor <user_id> --since 2026-09-01 --until 2026-09-30 --json
```

Each user gets one line: the days with activity, the distinct UTC hours with activity, the number of events, the last day, and one character per day (taller means more active hours).

| Flag | Filters on |
| --- | --- |
| `--since`, `--until` | UTC days, inclusive. A date, an ISO-8601 instant, or a span such as `6w`. Default: the last 28 days. At most 400 days. |
| `--client <c>` | `browser`: a person in a web page (a `Mozilla/` user agent). `program`: the CLI, the SDK, a sandbox, or a script acting as the user. |
| `--project`, `--actor`, `--actor-type`, `--credential-kind` | As for `kortix audit ls` |

The API route is `GET /v1/accounts/:accountId/audit/activity`, with `since`, `until`, `project_id`, `actor`, `client`, `actor_type` and `credential_kind`. It needs the same `audit.read` permission and entitlement as the log. A background job adds each closed 15-minute window of the log to the rollup about 2 hours after it closes. `rolled_through` in the response is the instant the counts reach. The rollup covers the last 90 days of the log.

### Retention and export

| Age of an event | Where it lives | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 90 days | The database | The web app, `kortix audit ls`, the API, webhooks, export |
| 90 to 365 days | A locked archive that nobody can shorten or delete | Export only |
| More than 365 days | Deleted | Not available |

```bash
kortix audit export --since 180d --format jsonl --out audit.jsonl
kortix audit export --action iam. --format csv --out iam.csv
```

Export takes the same filters, returns events oldest first, and reads the archive and the database as one list. Over the API: `GET /v1/accounts/:accountId/audit/export?format=csv|jsonl`, 10,000 events per page. Follow the `X-Audit-Next-Cursor` header until `X-Audit-Complete` is `true`.

### Stream to a SIEM

An audit webhook POSTs every new event to your endpoint. Managing webhooks needs `account.write`. Creating, enabling or disabling one also needs the entitlement. Deleting one never does.

```bash
kortix audit webhooks add --name siem --url https://siem.example.com/kortix --action-prefix iam.
kortix audit webhooks ls
kortix audit webhooks disable <webhook_id>
kortix audit webhooks enable <webhook_id>
kortix audit webhooks rm <webhook_id>
```

`add` prints the signing secret (`whsec_…`) once, and sends a test event at once. `--action-prefix` limits delivery to matching actions. The URL must be `http` or `https` and must not resolve to a private address.

Each delivery is a `POST` with the body `{"schema_version": 1, "event": {…}}` and these headers:

| Header | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `X-Kortix-Signature` | `sha256=` and the hex HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body, keyed with the signing secret |
| `X-Kortix-Idempotency-Key` | A stable key per webhook and event. Use it to drop duplicates. |
| `X-Kortix-Webhook-Id` | The webhook |
| `X-Kortix-Event` | `audit` |

Verify a delivery before you trust it:

```ts
import { createHmac, timingSafeEqual } from 'node:crypto';

function verify(rawBody: string, header: string, secret: string): boolean {
  const expected = 'sha256=' + createHmac('sha256', secret).update(rawBody).digest('hex');
  return header.length === expected.length && timingSafeEqual(Buffer.from(header), Buffer.from(expected));
}
```

A `2xx` response within 5 seconds is a success. Kortix retries any other result up to 8 attempts, waiting 30 seconds and doubling up to 1 hour between attempts. After the last attempt the delivery is `dead_letter`. Read deliveries with `GET /v1/accounts/:accountId/audit/webhooks/:webhookId/deliveries?status=<status>`, and send one again with `POST .../deliveries/:deliveryId/replay`.

### Action names

Every action is `domain.resource.verb`, and each API route has exactly one. Filter by prefix: `iam.` for access changes, `git.` for clones and pushes (`git.clone`, `git.push`; a push lists its refs in `metadata.refs`). A request that matched no route is `api.route.unmatched`. [Audit actions](/docs/audit-actions) lists every action and its title.

## Permissions

A permission is one action, such as `project.secret.write`. Roles are sets of permissions. Read the live catalog with `kortix permissions ls`, filter with `--scope account|project` or `--area <area>`, and open one with `kortix permissions show <action>`. Over the API: `GET /v1/accounts/:accountId/iam/permissions`, which also returns each permission's `implies` list.

**Level** is `view`, `edit` or `admin`.

## Leave or delete an account

### Leave

Open **Account settings** → **Members**, open the menu on your own row, and select **Leave account**. You lose access to the account and its projects. Your computers stop serving the account, and your reminders in its projects pause. See [When a person leaves](#when-a-person-leaves). The last owner cannot leave (`409`): make another member owner first.

### Delete

Deletion needs `account.delete`, which only an owner holds. It acts on your primary account: the first account you joined, normally the personal account Kortix created at sign-up. Open **Settings** → **Profile** and select **Delete account**. Pick one:

- **Grace period.** Kortix deletes the account 14 days later. You can cancel any time before then.
- **Delete immediately.** Kortix deletes the account at once. This cannot be undone.

Deletion removes:

- the account's sandboxes, session files and Kortix-managed repositories;
- every project and session in the account, with its secrets and connector accounts;
- the subscription, which Kortix cancels, and the remaining credits;
- for your personal account, your login, your devices and your notifications.

The deletion routes are under `/v1/account/` in the [API reference](https://api.kortix.com/v1/docs).

To leave a team account you do not own, use [Leave](#leave) instead.
